# Cross-interview synthesis memo

Lead-researcher rollup across all nine interviews. Themes are ordered by
breadth of support; attributions use persona ids. "Stated" = explicitly
asked for; "latent" = inferred with a documented basis (see per-interview
memos for each inference basis).

## Recurring themes

**S1. The exposure denominator is the single unanimous gap — and an
imperfect, loudly-labeled proxy is wanted *now*.** All nine participants hit
it. Experts frame it methodologically (`nl-network-planner`: "where crashes
happened to riders who happened to be there"; `us-bna-analyst`: "a ridership
map, not a risk map"), operators frame it as the sentence they need in a
room (`chi-ward-office`: "ridership roughly doubled and crashes didn't";
`chi-pro-advocate`: "ridership on this block is not zero" — the 18th Street
loss), and the resident frames it in plain language (`chi-everyday-rider`:
"per rider, is my street actually risky, or is it just busy"). Critically,
four participants independently *rejected* waiting for perfect data
(`dk-kpi-strategist` twice, `chi-cdot-planner`, `uk-ward-campaigner`,
`chi-ward-office`): a Divvy-trip proxy labeled as a floor clears their bar.
Two guardrails attach: the proxy's demographic/geographic skew must be
caveated (`uk-ward-campaigner`, `us-bna-analyst`), and a low number must not
be presentable as a reason to disinvest (`chi-everyday-rider` latent,
`chi-community-organizer`'s framing check).

**S2. The mock obstruction layer is the site's biggest trust liability —
unanimous, deal-breaker grade.** Every participant distrusts it; five name
it a deal-breaker. The badge system does not save it because **badges don't
survive screenshots** (`chi-cdot-planner`'s URL-cropped-screenshot incident;
`nl-network-planner`; `chi-everyday-rider` "I'm not hovering over anything,
I'm on my phone"). Remedies proposed range from click-through gating
(`chi-ward-office`, recurring not one-time) through visually-incompatible
rendering (`us-bna-analyst`) to full removal from map surfaces
(`dk-kpi-strategist`: "a toggle implies parity"; `chi-community-organizer`:
"get the real Bike Lane Uprising numbers or don't show me a map at all").
Corollary: real crowdsourced data would be *more* trusted than official data
by the resident (`chi-everyday-rider` trusts a fellow rider's report over a
city inspector).

**S3. Provenance, caveats, and freshness must travel with the artifact, not
live on the page.** The consumption unit is a screenshot, a printout, a CSV
row, a forwarded PDF — not a browser tab. (`chi-pro-advocate` latent:
caveats stripped on export reintroduce her defensibility gap;
`chi-everyday-rider` latent: everything reaches her network as a cropped
screenshot in a group chat; `chi-community-organizer`: paper is the
requirement for the room; `nl-network-planner` & `chi-community-organizer`
latent: per-number "as of" dates; `chi-ward-office` latent: point-in-time
snapshots so a number cited in March can be reconciled in August.)

**S4. The ward one-pager is the killer artifact.** Six participants
independently described nearly the same weekly, ward-scoped, one-page
deliverable: `chi-pro-advocate` (her quarterly ward letter, automated),
`chi-ward-office` (Monday-briefing folder, "one-pager, front only"),
`chi-community-organizer` (block-captains group text + alderman's liaison),
`chi-everyday-rider` (her streets + one alderman fact + one action),
`chi-cdot-planner` (pre-built corridor packet for the exposed room),
`uk-ward-campaigner` (croppable leaflet artifact). Three of them also
independently split it by audience: persuasion leave-behind vs. Q&A backup
packet (`chi-community-organizer`), alderwoman one-pager vs. merchant
economic packet (`chi-ward-office`), press-facing vs. resident-facing
register (`chi-pro-advocate`).

**S5. Derived scores are distrusted in exact proportion to their opacity.**
The 0–100 ward safety index drew distrust or misreading from every expert:
components must be separable (`us-bna-analyst`'s bivariate view), weighting
public and defensible (`nl-network-planner` via Fietsbalans), severity
weighting expected à la CDOT's High Injury Network (`chi-pro-advocate`,
`chi-cdot-planner`), window/stability disclosed (`us-bna-analyst`), and the
direction unambiguous (`chi-community-organizer` read higher-as-safer).
`chi-ward-office` adds the political dimension: a ward ranking is a press
liability first, an insight second. Nobody asked for the blend; several
asked for the components.

**S6. Legislation needs two-stage status; hearings are the sleeper hit.**
`chi-pro-advocate` calls a collapsed status field "actively dangerous"
(Feb 2026 committee-pass/floor-fail); `chi-ward-office` needs committee
roll-calls to whip votes. Meanwhile the hearings calendar was the least
conditional positive in the study (`chi-pro-advocate`, `chi-ward-office`,
`chi-cdot-planner`, `chi-community-organizer` — "I don't even need
convincing").

**S7. Proxy layers need reframing, not just badges.** 311 = "loud vs.
actually dangerous" (`chi-ward-office` keeps a hand-built spreadsheet solely
to make that distinction; `chi-community-organizer` lives the undercount
side). Cameras read as revenue extraction, not safety, to exactly the
audiences OYL wants to serve (`chi-everyday-rider` "money grab",
`chi-community-organizer` "a collection machine with a badge on it" — usable
only if reframed as enforcement-equity data; `nl-network-planner` wants them
out of the safety narrative).

**S8. The resident surface is a different product register.** Route-level
("my three streets"), phone-first, plain-language, action-bundled
(`chi-everyday-rider` throughout; `chi-community-organizer`'s address-search
need — "half my room doesn't know what ward they're in"). Jargon is an
audience filter: "KSI" reads as "not for me." Fear without an action loses
her; the *good route*, not the danger catalog, is the question. The findings
cards and CSV exports that experts love are invisible or hostile to her.

**S9. Gaps and network structure, not just corridors.**
`nl-network-planner`'s core artifact is the missing-link map (machine
candidates acceptable as hypothesis generators only); `uk-ward-campaigner`
needs a demand/route-potential justification for non-flagship streets;
`us-bna-analyst` wants stress/connectivity, teachable to lay audiences;
`chi-everyday-rider`'s top signal is protected-vs-paint at route level.

**S10. Accountability data should pair activity with outcomes and promises
with delivery.** Sponsorship ≠ votes (loud labeling: `uk-ward-campaigner`);
sponsorship + before/after outcomes (`nl-network-planner`);
promised-vs-delivered per ward is the single biggest hours-saver for the
insider (`chi-ward-office`, partially replaceable only — his "scar sheet"
provenance tiers stay his); menu-money figures are unusable publicly until
verified against OBM source PDFs (`chi-cdot-planner`, `chi-community-organizer`,
`chi-ward-office` all independently).

## Tensions

- **Event cadence vs. freshness**: `dk-kpi-strategist` wants a rare "annual
  moment"; operators want same-day/weekly. Resolution: both — weekly data
  currency plus one annual editorial publication (a "Chicago bike account").
- **Danger visibility vs. stigma**: advocates need the danger map;
  `chi-community-organizer` fears "Austin's a war zone" framing and
  `chi-everyday-rider` may respond to crash dots by driving. Resolution:
  danger data must ship welded to cause/context (infrastructure absence,
  investment history) and to an action.
- **Single authoritative number vs. component transparency**: politicians
  and rooms want one number (`dk-kpi-strategist`'s headline chart); every
  analyst wants the components. Resolution: headline artifacts pair TWO
  numbers (progress + harm), while every derived number links to components.
- **City-verified vs. community-sourced trust**: `chi-everyday-rider` trusts
  a rider photo over an inspector; experts require official provenance.
  The badge system already models this — it needs to be legible per audience.

## Needs unique to one participant but strategically important

- `chi-cdot-planner`'s install-date history (enables every before/after
  claim everyone else wants; OYL's snapshot mechanism is the only public
  path to it).
- `dk-kpi-strategist`'s "metric needs a named owner and a target" — no one
  in Chicago owns a number; OYL can at least display *stated* city targets
  (Cycling Strategy: 150 mi / 80% low-stress; Vision Zero) against actuals.
- `chi-everyday-rider`'s "confirmation moment" insight — the act of
  documenting is the need; don't build tracking.
- `chi-community-organizer`'s single-author contribution model — never
  crowd-editable.

## What this method cannot tell us

Simulated participants cannot reveal real usability failures (only real
sessions can), actual Chicago political dynamics beyond documented cases,
true task time-costs, or genuine misreading rates. Several persona episodes
are invented texture around documented practice — treat quotes as evidence
of *need shape*, not of fact. Specific low-confidence items are flagged in
each memo's confidence_notes (e.g., the pro-advocate's exposure-source list,
the ward chief's scar-sheet precision, the everyday rider's Divvy
improvisation). The strongest findings are those stated independently by
3+ participants from different worlds and corroborated by the cited
evidence briefs; every proposal in the UX report is gated on that standard.
Follow-up with real humans is recommended before major investment:
an ATA staffer, a ward office, two everyday riders, and Bike Lane Uprising.
