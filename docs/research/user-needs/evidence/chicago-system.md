# Evidence Brief: Chicago's Real Bike-Safety Advocacy Machinery

## 1. Who the real actors are and how they operate

**Aldermen and "aldermanic prerogative."** Each of Chicago's 50 wards gets a fixed capital allotment — commonly cited at **$1.5M/ward/year** (some ward sites cite $1.3M) — the "menu money" program, run through the Office of Budget and Management, which alders can spend on CDOT's pre-priced menu of standard projects (repaving, curb ramps, bike lanes, traffic calming) *or* non-menu asks (Alderman Bennett Lawson's 44th Ward site, Alderman Matt Martin's 46th Ward site; CTBA blog, "What does aldermanic 'menu money' get spent on?"). Since a 2019 Lightfoot executive order *nominally* ended "aldermanic privilege," CDOT officials by that same November had already acknowledged alders retain de facto veto power over infrastructure in their wards (Streetsblog Chicago, Aug 2023, "Commissioner Biagi and Ald. La Spata discuss how aldermanic prerogative affects bikeway installation"). The clearest recent case: in **Oct–Nov 2025** Ald. Pat Dowell (3rd Ward) ordered CDOT to pause and **rip out already-installed protected bike lane infrastructure, including a protected intersection, on 18th Street** between Clark and Indiana after opening it to renewed community objections (Streetsblog Chicago, Nov 21, 2025). Ald. Raymond Lopez (15th) has similarly fought the Archer Avenue Complete Streets project (Streetsblog/multiple). Advocates and CDOT itself note aldermanic prerogative is a structural driver of why South/West Side wards have sparser, less-connected bike networks than the North Side.

**CDOT** runs the Bikeways/Complete Streets program under the 2006 Complete Streets Policy, codified in the **Complete Streets Design Guidelines** (2013, updated 2025) that set a modal hierarchy prioritizing pedestrians (chicago.gov/completestreets; NACTO-hosted PDF). CDOT's **Chicago Cycling Strategy** (released March 2023, first update since a 2012 plan) commits to 150 miles of new bikeways, 80% low-stress (protected lanes, neighborhood greenways, off-street trails), targeting 70% of residents within a half-mile of low-stress biking (chicago.gov press release; Streetsblog Chicago, Apr 11, 2023; Block Club Chicago, Apr 10, 2023 — advocates said it needed to be "far more aggressive"). CDOT delivers **Neighborhood Bike Networks** via community task forces (e.g., Austin, Belmont Cragin, North Lawndale — tripled bikeway mileage in two years working with local CBOs; chicagocompletestreets.org). Mayor Johnson/CDOT announced **100 miles of new bikeways** completed via the Milwaukee Avenue corridor project in Sept 2025 (chicago.gov press release).

**City Council Committee on Pedestrian and Traffic Safety**, chaired by Ald. Daniel La Spata, has jurisdiction over bike lanes, crosswalks, traffic ordinances (Chicago Councilmatic; the1stward.com/cpts). It passed a **Smart Streets Pilots ordinance (2023)** allowing camera enforcement of bus/bike lanes and loading zones, and in **Feb 2026** advanced (then saw nearly two-thirds of full Council vote down) an ordinance letting citizens submit phone-recorded evidence of commercial vehicles blocking bike/bus lanes (Streetsblog Chicago, Feb 19, 2026; WBEZ, Feb 10, 2026). City Bureau's **Open Gov Report Card gave the Committee a "D"** for transparency (meeting minutes/recording accessibility) — evidence that even the formal legislative venue for bike safety is opaque (citybureau.org).

**Active Transportation Alliance (ATA)** is the leading regional advocacy nonprofit. It sends **ward-by-ward letters to all 50 alderpeople with up-to-date crash data** (deaths/serious injuries by mode, by ward) — done two years running as of 2025/2026 (activetransportation.org, "The ward-by-ward effort to eliminate crashes"). It also tracks that **fatal crashes fell 30% citywide 2021–2024**, that **72% of 2024 fatalities involved speeding**, and that South/West Side wards carry disproportionate severe-crash concentrations. ATA publishes periodic **Regional Crash Reports** (2020 edition covering 2008–2017) showing walkers/bikers were 3.8% of commuters but 11% of crash injuries/deaths.

**Better Streets Chicago** (founded 2020 by Kyle Lucas, Brittney Lane, Jim Hodapp, and former Streetsblog editor Courtney Cobbs) is a grassroots 501(c)(3) focused on protected bike infrastructure, bus lanes, and open streets — organizes testimony, petitions, and an annual "year in advocacy" recap (Streetsblog Chicago, Nov 25, 2024; betterstreetschicago.org).

**Bike Lane Uprising** (founded by Christina Whitehouse) crowdsources bike-lane-obstruction reports via a mobile app; the database has grown from **25,000+ reports (2018–2021)** to **65,000+ reports** with a documented **$4.8M+ in associated ticket value** for the 2018–2021 window alone. Its data has been used in litigation after cyclist deaths/injuries, to lobby for ordinances, and to pressure specific companies that repeatedly block lanes (bikelaneuprising.com/insights; Northwestern Magazine profile; Streetsblog Chicago, Oct 29, 2020).

**Chi Hack Night / Ward Wise**, a civic-tech breakout group, independently scrapes and republishes aldermanic menu-spending data (sourced from OBM's PDF archive since 2012 plus a researcher's FOIA'd data back to 2005) and CDOT bikeway plans as an open API/interactive map, explicitly to make ward infrastructure spending "engaging and understandable" (chihacknight.org; wardwisechicago.org/faqs).

**Chicago BikePAC**, launched Aug 21, 2025 (co-founders Kevin Borgia and Chris Ridgeway, a former La Spata comms director), is a new political-action committee aiming to elect a bike-friendly City Council in 2027, endorsing on "cycling infrastructure, comprehensive traffic and safety policy, equitable enforcement" (Streetsblog Chicago, Aug 19 & 22, 2025).

**311/CHI311** has a dedicated code for "Vehicle Parking in a Bike Lane," but CDOT and 311 explicitly state complaints are used only for **aggregate hotspot identification, not individual ticketing** — a persistent point of confusion/frustration among cyclists and even conflicting statements from alderpeople (WTTW, Jun 16, 2022, "CDOT Says it Won't Pursue Citations"; 311.chicago.gov FAQ; WTTW, Dec 3, 2019, "Cyclists Say City Isn't Addressing 311 Complaints").

## 2. What data they actually use, publish, or demand

- **CDOT's High Injury Network (HIN)**: top 100 miles of streets identified from **3 years of crash data (2022–2024)**, weighting fatal/serious-injury crashes more heavily; feeds a "High Crash Corridors Framework Plan" (chicago.gov Data Resources page; CDOT Fall 2025 Traffic Crashes Annual Report).
- **City of Chicago Data Portal**: "Traffic Crashes – Crashes" and "Traffic Crashes – Vision Zero Chicago Traffic Fatalities" datasets (data.cityofchicago.org, gzaz-isa6 / 85ca-t3if), plus "Ped/Bike Crashes by Month." These are the canonical primary sources CDOT, ATA, and journalists all cite.
- **Vision Zero targets**: eliminate deaths/serious injuries by 2026 (set 2017); CDOT's newer Complete Streets Design Guide (2025) states goals to eliminate ped fatalities in 10 years and cut ped/bike injuries 50% in 5 years, with a 50%-of-trips-by-sustainable-modes target for 2030 (currently ~38%).
- **2024 outcome data**: 109 traffic deaths (38 pedestrians, 2 cyclists, 69 vehicle occupants); 23,000+ injuries in 2023 (8% above 2017) — cited as evidence Vision Zero's 2026 goal will be missed (Chicago Sun-Times, Aug 25, 2025).
- **Aldermanic menu-spending data**: itemized PDFs published quarterly by OBM since 2012, FOIA-extended back to 2005, scraped/normalized by Ward Wise and independent researchers/journalists (John Ruf, Jake Smith) — this is literally the dataset the persona's "aldermanic menu-money spending" data type mirrors.
- **Bike Lane Uprising obstruction data**: user-submitted reports with location, vehicle type/plate, ward, bike lane type; aggregated into maps/tables sortable by ward — directly analogous to the dashboard's MOCK obstruction layer.
- **Red-light/speed camera ticket data**: UIC study and Fines & Fees Justice Center report found South Side cameras issue **~2.5–3x more tickets per camera** than North Side; Black/Latino ZIP codes ticketed at ~2x the rate of white ZIP codes; low-income residents incur fees on 46% of tickets vs. 17% for higher-income (ProPublica-cited; Illinois Policy Institute, Oct 2024/2025; UIC Indigo repository).
- **CDOT's 2026 economic-impact study**: examined six corridors with bike lanes vs. "control" corridors, tracking sales-tax revenue, commercial vacancy, employment, and safety/ridership pre/post-installation — found no harm to business, sales tax recovered post-COVID to baseline (Block Club Chicago, Jun 23, 2026; Streetsblog Chicago, May 19, 2026). This is a rare case of a rigorous CDOT-commissioned study directly countering a recurring merchant objection.
- **Participatory budgeting**: pioneered in the 49th Ward (2009, Ald. Joe Moore), later 5th, 45th, 46th wards; residents vote directly on menu-money projects at expos — a subset of wards apply direct democracy to bike/ped spending while most don't (Great Cities Institute/UIC report; The TRiiBE, Jul 2025, "why aren't many Black wards doing it?").

## 3. Documented wins/failures where data played a role

- **Win**: CDOT's 2021 Austin/Belmont Cragin/North Lawndale task-force process (community-identified destinations/barriers) tripled bikeway mileage in those neighborhoods in two years — evidence that community-sourced, not just crash, data moves projects.
- **Win**: Milwaukee Avenue protected lanes were installed after two cyclist deaths were cited as the direct trigger (Block Club Chicago, Jun 2022) — a clear case of fatality data forcing action.
- **Failure/backslide**: Ald. Dowell's 2025 removal of installed 18th Street protection despite CDOT's design work already complete — shows data/design work can be overridden by aldermanic prerogative regardless of evidence.
- **Failure**: Feb 2026 City Council voted down (nearly 2/3 against) the citizen bike/bus-lane-blockage reporting ordinance even after committee passage — shows crash/obstruction data and committee-level consensus don't guarantee full-Council follow-through.
- **Mixed**: 311 bike-lane-blockage reports are collected at scale but explicitly *not* wired to enforcement, a long-running (2019–present) credibility gap between "data collected" and "data acted on."
- **Win (counter-narrative)**: CDOT's 2026 economic study countered the recurring "bike lanes kill business" narrative (e.g., the 2022 Logan Square shop-closure story) with sales-tax and vacancy data across six corridors.

## 4. Implications for the persona

A person embedded in this world — whether a ward-office staffer, CDOT planner, ATA/Better Streets organizer, or Chi Hack Night volunteer — would:
- Talk fluently in **wards, menu money, and prerogative** rather than abstract "policy," and instinctively ask "which alderman controls this block" before "what does the data say."
- Want **ward-level rollups and comparisons** (crash counts, menu $ spent on safety vs. paving, 311 volume) because that's literally the currency ATA and Ward Wise already use to pressure alderpeople.
- Be skeptical of **new, mocked, or crowdsourced layers** (like a Bike Lane Uprising-style obstruction layer) unless provenance and update cadence are crystal clear — this world has been burned by "data collected but not actionable" (311) and cares about whether a metric can survive a hearing or a lawsuit.
- Want to see **committee/legislative status and sponsorship** tied to concrete outcomes (did the ordinance pass, who voted no) because that's the theory of change ATA, Better Streets, and BikePAC all operate on.
- Have latent frustration with a **"danger index" style single number** — CDOT's real HIN methodology weights by severity, not raw counts, and practitioners would probe how any derived index is computed and whether it double-counts or hides ward-level equity disparities (as red-light-camera-equity research shows raw enforcement data can mask racial/geographic disparities).
- Care about **menu-money "what got funded vs. what got promised"** transparency — the whole point of Ward Wise's project was that this wasn't otherwise auditable.

## Data-need signals (analyst-extracted)

- Ward-level rollups need to be comparable to ATA's own ward-by-ward crash-data letters (deaths/serious injuries by mode, by ward) so users can cross-check the dashboard against advocacy materials they already trust.
- Any derived 'danger index' should expose its weighting methodology (severity-weighted like CDOT's High Injury Network, using 3-year crash windows) rather than presenting a single opaque score, since practitioners are primed to distrust unexplained indices.
- Aldermanic menu-money spending data needs a 'promised vs. actually spent on safety/bike infrastructure' breakdown, mirroring the gap Ward Wise/Chi Hack Night built their whole project to expose.
- 311 bike-complaint data should be clearly labeled as a hotspot/volume proxy, not an enforcement-outcome proxy, given the well-documented 2019-2022 credibility gap between 311 submissions and actual citations.
- Speed/red-light camera data should support ward or ZIP-level equity comparisons (tickets per camera, income/race correlation) since that is the dominant published framing of this data (UIC study, Fines and Fees Justice Center, Illinois Policy Institute).
- Legislation/sponsorship tracking needs committee-level AND full-Council vote stages distinguished, since the Feb 2026 bike/bus lane ordinance passed committee but failed full Council — a single 'status' field would misrepresent outcomes.
- The mock bike-lane-obstruction layer should be presented with explicit provenance/freshness caveats and a clear 'not yet Bike Lane Uprising data' disclaimer, since users familiar with the real BLU dataset (65,000+ reports) will expect ward-sortable, ticket-value-linked detail.
- Route report cards (offstreet/protected/painted/none) should allow linking a segment to its aldermanic ward and menu-money history, since infrastructure grade and political/funding history are treated as inseparable by this audience.
- Include a 'business impact' or economic-indicator toggle for corridors, since CDOT's 2026 sales-tax/vacancy study is the primary rebuttal advocates now cite against the 'bike lanes hurt business' objection.
- Participatory-budgeting flag at the ward level (which wards let residents vote on menu-money projects) would let users see which wards have a structurally different, more transparent path to bike-safety funding.
