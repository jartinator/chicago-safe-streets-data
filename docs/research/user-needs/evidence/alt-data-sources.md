## Evidence Brief: Alternative Data Sources in the Chicago Bike-Safety Landscape

### 1. Big-data mobility platforms (Strava Metro, StreetLight, Replica)

**Strava Metro** is genuinely free for public agencies and "safe-infrastructure advocates" — Strava made Metro free for urban planners, city governments, and DOTs in September 2020 ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/carltonreid/2020/09/23/strava-metro-data-service-gifted-to-cities-to-boost-bicycling/); [Bicycle Retailer](https://www.bicycleretailer.com/industry-news/2020/09/23/strava-metro-now-free-urban-planners-and-advocates)). Roughly 3,500 organizations use it, including DOTs and the government of Catalonia ([metro.strava.com FAQ](https://metro.strava.com/faq)). Caveat: it's a self-selected sample of Strava app users — skews toward recreational/athletic, higher-income riders logging "exercise" rides, not the utilitarian/low-income commuting trips a danger-index tool most needs — a well-known bias in the planning literature.

**StreetLight Data** derives bike/ped volumes from mobile-phone location data at any census tract "within minutes," marketed specifically as a bike/ped alternative to physical counters ([StreetLight](https://www.streetlightdata.com/bicycle-and-pedestrian-metrics-alternative-to-iot-bike-counters/); [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/analytics/streetlight-data-carves-out-bike-and-pedestrian-travel-tool.html)). **Replica** builds activity-based synthetic-population travel models used by agencies for corridor/safety prioritization ([Replica](https://replicahq.com/); [Replica blog on safe network design](https://www.replicahq.com/post/from-data-to-design-creating-safer-more-connected-active-transportation-networks)). Both are enterprise SaaS with custom, contact-us pricing and no public nonprofit tier found ([StreetLight pricing](https://www.streetlightdata.com/pricing/)) — realistically a **long-shot partnership**, not a near-term win, unless CDOT or CMAP already holds a license the project could piggyback on.

### 2. Chicago's own crash-data ecosystem, and its gaps

The Chicago Data Portal's Traffic Crashes datasets are sourced from CPD's E-Crash system and follow the state SR1050 form, but explicitly **exclude** crashes on expressways/ramps and any crash where CPD wasn't the responding agency, and exclude bike "dooring" incidents from the reportability threshold used elsewhere in Illinois statute ([Chicago Data Portal crash data documentation](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/Traffic-Crashes-Vehicles/68nd-jvt3/data); [IDOT Crash Data](https://idot.illinois.gov/travel-and-maps/safety/crash.html)). IDOT's statewide crash database is the superset and the two can diverge on jurisdiction and update timing. CDOT publishes its own annual Chicago Traffic Crashes report and operates a Vision Zero Data Dashboard with a mapped **High Injury Network** ([ArcGIS HIN dashboard](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/74363e0dbb3e43138bc7d451a90817ef/page/High-Injury-Network/?views=High-Injury-Network); [GitHub Chicago/vision-zero-dashboard](https://github.com/Chicago/vision-zero-dashboard)); a Sun-Times investigation (Aug 2025) found the city is "not even close" to its 2026 Vision Zero elimination target ([Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/transportation/2025/08/25/vision-zero-traffic-crash-deaths-pedestrian-cyclist-car-roads-streets)) — a documented failure where the dashboard *existed* but political follow-through lagged.

### 3. Injury surveillance beyond police crash reports

Illinois Department of Public Health runs a statewide **Trauma Registry** (Level I/II trauma centers must report) and a **Prehospital/EMS Data Program** feeding the national NEMSIS repository ([IDPH Injury-Related Data](https://dph.illinois.gov/topics-services/prevention-wellness/injury-violence-prevention/injury-related-data.html); [IDPH EMS Reporting System](https://www.idph.state.il.us/emsrpt/crash.asp)). This is the classic "hidden morbidity" fix — police crash reports systematically undercount cyclist injuries (especially solo falls, dooring, and injuries where the cyclist declines police involvement); EMS/trauma data would catch these but access requires a data-use agreement with IDPH, not an open download — a **medium-term ask**, not a scrape.

**Cook County Medical Examiner** publishes a public, dataset-level **Case Archive** (Aug 2014–present, updated daily, open on the Cook County open-data portal) that includes manner-of-death coding and could be filtered for traffic/cyclist fatalities as a fatality-only cross-check against CPD crash records ([Cook County Medical Examiner Case Archive](https://datacatalog.cookcountyil.gov/Public-Safety/Medical-Examiner-Case-Archive/cjeq-bs86)) — this is a **realistic near-term win**, it's already open data.

### 4. Infrastructure inventory and crowdsourced auditing

**CMAP** (Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning) maintains a regional **Bikeway Inventory System (BIS)** geodatabase across the seven-county area and runs the periodic **My Daily Travel** household travel survey, whose newest wave (Sept 2024–June 2025) was just published with public-use microdata on mode choice and trip purpose ([CMAP Data Hub BIS](https://datahub.cmap.illinois.gov/datasets/CMAPGIS::bikeway-inventory-system-bis?layer=60); [CMAP travel survey](https://cmap.illinois.gov/data/transportation/travel-survey/)) — both open, both a strong near-term complement to CDOT's own bikeway layer, especially for suburb-adjacent wards.

**OpenStreetMap** bike-lane completeness is inconsistent nationally and quality varies heavily by locality/mapper density, though academic comparisons (e.g., Ferster et al.-style studies referenced via [ScienceDirect: Using OpenStreetMap to inventory bicycle infrastructure](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/abs/pii/S1556831822002763)) find OSM length concordance with municipal data ranges from <±2% to ±30% depending on the city; Chicago's OSM wiki page flags bike lanes/trails as an area still needing systematic tagging ([OSM Wiki: Chicago, Illinois](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois)) — usable as a cross-check on the dashboard's own facility-category report cards, not a sole source. **Mapillary** crowdsourced street imagery (CC BY-SA, open API) is used in academic work to auto-generate bikeway networks and audit infrastructure by computer vision ("Towards generating network of bikeways from Mapillary data," [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0198971521000399)) — a genuinely **volunteer-project-feasible** near-term win since it's open and API-accessible, unlike proprietary street view.

### 5. Adjacent advocacy/enforcement data actors

**Active Transportation Alliance** sends Chicago's 50 wards annual letters with up-to-date fatality/serious-injury data broken out by mode and invites alderpersons to meet — a direct precedent for a ward-facing danger-index product ([ATA ward-by-ward effort](https://activetransportation.org/blog/the-ward-by-ward-effort-to-reduce-walking-biking-crashes/)). ATA reports 2021–2024 fatalities fell 30% citywide but severe crashes remain concentrated on South/West Side arterials, and that 72% of 2024 fatalities involved speeding — this is the kind of framing ("evidence-based... yet inequitable outcomes") a persona from this world would use natively.

**Bike Lane Uprising**, the org named in the dashboard's own pending-partnership mock layer, is real: founded in Chicago, crowdsourced via a mobile app, now active in 100+ cities, and its data has been used in court cases, to pressure companies directly, and reportedly to help pass legislation ([WTTW](https://news.wttw.com/2020/10/27/new-app-lets-cyclists-report-bike-lane-obstructions-hazards); [Bike Lane Uprising Insights](https://www.bikelaneuprising.com/insights); [ATA blog on BLU](https://activetransportation.org/blog/crowdsourcing-safer-bike-lanes/)) — confirms the mocked layer models a real, plausible partnership target.

Court/citation data for enforcement exists but is fragmented: Cook County's Circuit Court Clerk runs an e-Citation lookup system and Chicago's Data Portal separately publishes Speed Camera Violations and Ordinance Violations datasets, but there is no unified, open dataset joining moving-violation citations to adjudication outcomes ([Cook County e-Citation](https://www.cookcountyclerkofcourt.org/e-citation); [Chicago Speed Camera Violations](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/Speed-Camera-Violations/hhkd-xvj4)) — likely FOIA territory, a **long-shot** for a volunteer project.

Insurance telematics is essentially auto-only in the literature found (usage-based-insurance crash-risk studies); no evidence of a bicycle-specific telematics dataset accessible to municipalities or advocates — **flag as unverified/not viable**, omit from realistic roadmap.

### Implications for the persona

This world's actors are fluent in the vocabulary of "denominators" (trips vs. crashes), sampling bias caveats, ward-level accountability letters, and jurisdictional edge cases (CPD-responding vs. not, dooring exclusions). They are chronically frustrated by data that is either too proprietary (StreetLight/Replica quotes require "contact us"), too incomplete (police-only crash counts miss injuries), or too political (Vision Zero dashboards that don't translate into results, per the Sun-Times). Their latent need is less "more data" than **triangulation and provenance-labeling** — a way to visibly mark which numbers are official/verified (CDOT, IDOT, ME) vs. modeled/proprietary (StreetLight, Replica) vs. crowdsourced/self-selected (Strava, Bike Lane Uprising, OSM) so advocacy claims survive scrutiny from skeptical alderpersons and press.

## Data-need signals (analyst-extracted)

- The dashboard should visibly label each data layer's provenance tier (official/government, licensed-proprietary, or crowdsourced/self-selected) so advocacy claims survive scrutiny.
- A cross-check layer combining Cook County Medical Examiner fatality records against CPD crash data would surface undercounted cyclist deaths.
- A ward-level 'letter to alderman' export mirroring Active Transportation Alliance's annual ward crash-data letters would map directly onto known advocacy workflows.
- Surfacing IDOT vs. Chicago Data Portal jurisdictional differences (expressways, non-CPD-responding crashes, dooring exclusions) would preempt a common data-accuracy critique from informed users.
- An OpenStreetMap/Mapillary-based crowdsourced audit overlay could supplement or validate CDOT's own bikeway facility-category report cards, especially in under-mapped wards.
- A 'confidence/coverage' indicator on the danger index would help users understand where EMS/trauma-registry-level injury undercounting (vs. police-report-only data) most likely biases the index.
- Flagging that big-data platforms (Strava Metro, StreetLight, Replica) carry demographic sampling bias (recreational/higher-income riders) is needed wherever ridership-volume denominators are used to normalize crash risk.
- A status indicator distinguishing 'open dataset available today' (CMAP BIS, Cook County ME, Divvy trips) from 'requires partnership/paid license' (StreetLight, Replica, Bike Lane Uprising, IDPH trauma data) would set realistic roadmap expectations for volunteer contributors.
