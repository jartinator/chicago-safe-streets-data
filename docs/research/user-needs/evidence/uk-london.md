# Evidence Brief: UK/London Cycling Campaigning as a Ward-Level Data-Politics Model

## 1. Who the real actors are and how they operate

**London Cycling Campaign (LCC)** is a ~12,000-member charity (LCC's own membership figures, cited via Cyclescape documentation) organized as a federation of ~30 semi-autonomous **borough groups** (e.g., Camden Cyclists, Southwark Cyclists, Waterbeach CC, Haringey Cycling Campaign) that each run locally on Cyclescape.org. This structure is the key mechanism: national asks get translated into hyper-local, addressable demands by volunteers who know their own streets.

LCC's flagship tactic is **"Space for Cycling"** (2013–14): rather than one citywide ask, LCC mapped a specific infrastructure demand for *every one of London's 624 electoral wards*, built an online postcode-lookup tool so residents could see their ward's ask and email their candidates directly, and tracked candidate responses publicly. Reported outcome: 50% of candidates pledged support, translating to 47% of councillors actually elected in 2014 backing a specific local ask ([road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/115807-lcc-maps-every-ward-london-lobby-local-election-candidates-space-cycling); [Cycling UK](https://www.cyclinguk.org/news/space-for-cycling-launched-in-london)).

LCC's current iteration is **"Climate Safe Streets"** (run ahead of the 2022 and 2026 borough elections), which produces a borough-specific PDF of roughly five asks per borough (e.g., school streets at every school, protected cycle lanes on all TfL Strategic Cycling Analysis corridors by a named year) and a parallel **"Streets for Cycling"** pledge asking councils to commit to delivering all TfL-identified high-potential cycling corridors by 2030. LCC reports the 2022 round generated commitments across multiple boroughs, and the 2026 round produced "15+ councils likely committed to rapid cycle network delivery" ([lcc.org.uk](https://lcc.org.uk/news/local-elections-2026-a-big-win-for-london-cycling/); [lcc.org.uk/campaigns/climate-safe-streets](https://lcc.org.uk/campaigns/climate-safe-streets/)). One borough round alone (2022) generated nearly 11,500 emails from residents to candidates ([WebSearch summary of LCC news](https://lcc.org.uk/news/local-elections-2022-campaign-wins-commitments-to-cycling-climate/)).

**Transport for London (TfL)**, the statutory transport authority, is the primary data supplier campaigners rely on: it runs 60 automatic cycle counters on the Transport for London Road Network (TLRN), converting raw counts into an index of cycling growth over time; it publishes the **Strategic Cycling Analysis (SCA)** — a route-potential map originally built on 2014 cycle-trip data — that LCC explicitly cites as the basis for its "corridors" asks; and it publishes an annually updated **Cycling Action Plan** (CAP2, 2025) setting network-expansion targets ([content.tfl.gov.uk/cycling-action-plan.pdf](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/cycling-action-plan.pdf); [content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-strategic-cycling-analysis.pdf](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-strategic-cycling-analysis.pdf)).

**STATS19** is the UK's official police-reported road-casualty database (dating to 1979), the backbone of all UK cyclist-safety data. TfL publishes validated STATS19-derived collision dashboards and annual "Casualties in Greater London" factsheets (e.g., the 2024 report: 24,019 people injured on London roads in 2024, 1 cyclist death per 62 million cyclist journeys) ([tfl.gov.uk/corporate/publications-and-reports/road-safety](https://tfl.gov.uk/corporate/publications-and-reports/road-safety); [casualties-in-greater-london-2024.pdf](https://tfl.gov.uk/cdn/static/cms/documents/casualties-in-greater-london-2024.pdf)). Independent tools like the `stats19` R package (rOpenSci/ITS Leeds) and **CycleStreets' Collision Data Reports** repackage this same official feed for public/campaigner use ([itsleeds.github.io/stats19](https://itsleeds.github.io/stats19/); [cyclestreets.net/collisions](https://www.cyclestreets.net/collisions/)).

**CycleStreets** (not-for-profit, Cambridge-based, launched 2009) runs the national OSM-based journey planner, a crowdsourced hazard "Photomap," and **Cyclescape** — the campaigner toolkit LCC's borough groups use to log street-level issues, discuss consultations, and produce prioritized, evidenced improvement proposals for councils ([cyclestreets.net/about](https://www.cyclestreets.net/about/); [blog.cyclescape.org](https://blog.cyclescape.org/2011/05/06/campaigner-toolkit-backed-by-geovation/)).

A coalition of eight NGOs (LCC, Sustrans in London, Possible, London Living Streets, Wheels for Wellbeing, Action Vision Zero, CPRE London, Future Transport London) jointly publishes the **Healthy Streets Scorecard**, an annual ranked scoring of all 33 London boroughs on ten weighted indicators — six "input" measures (LTN schemes, 20mph limits, controlled parking zones, protected cycle tracks, School Streets) and four "outcome" measures (sustainable mode share, active-travel participation rate, serious-collision rate for people walking/cycling, car ownership) — combined into a single 0–10 borough score ([healthystreetsscorecard.london](https://www.healthystreetsscorecard.london/); [Trust for London](https://trustforlondon.org.uk/data/healthy-streets-scorecard/)).

Beyond London: **Paris en Selle** is a Paris-specific advocacy group that campaigns using results from the **Baromètre des Villes Cyclables**, a biennial national cyclist-perception survey run by the federation **FUB (Fédération française des usagers de la bicyclette)** under its "Parlons Vélo" brand — Paris scored a "C" (3.67/5) and ranked 11th nationally in 2025, up from a "D" in 2021 ([parisenselle.fr/barometre-des-villes-cyclables](https://parisenselle.fr/barometre-des-villes-cyclables/); [barometre.parlons-velo.fr](https://barometre.parlons-velo.fr/)). Germany's **ADFC (Allgemeiner Deutscher Fahrrad-Club)** runs the **Fahrradklima-Test**, surveying ~213,000 cyclists across 1,047 municipalities in its 2024 edition and publishing city-by-city rankings by population tier ([fahrradklima-test.adfc.de/ergebnisse](https://fahrradklima-test.adfc.de/ergebnisse); [ECF summary](https://ecf.com/news-and-events/news/adfc-fahrradklima-test-what-best-cycling-city-germany)). Seville's 2006–2007 rapid buildout (77km of protected track in under two years, ~€35M invested, cycling mode share rising from 0.6% to 9%, daily cycle trips rising from ~13,000 to ~72,000) is the standout "speed of delivery beats consultation paralysis" case study, widely cited by UCI, PeopleForBikes, and Streetsblog ([UCI](https://www.uci.org/article/seville-s-ambitious-bike-network-plan-a-success-and-growing/wLbZWoPI7UUFV7x9othoF); [PeopleForBikes](https://www.peopleforbikes.org/news/an-unsung-theme-of-sevillas-amazing-bike-network-legibility)).

## 2. What data is actually used, published, or demanded

Campaigners consistently demand and cite: (a) **route-potential/demand data** (TfL's SCA) to justify *where* corridors go; (b) **counter-derived flow indices** to show usage trends; (c) **STATS19 collision data**, filtered to pedal cyclists, to justify danger claims and target "high-casualty" locations; (d) **before/after traffic-volume counts** at LTN internal and boundary-road sites — the definitive 2023 study of London's LTNs found 73.5% of 412 internal count sites saw traffic decline (average 46.9% reduction) versus <1% net change on boundary roads across 175 sites, directly rebutting the "just displaces traffic" objection ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213624X23001785)); (e) **casualty-rate before/after comparisons**, e.g., Waltham Forest's roughly threefold casualty reduction post-LTN, and a broader study of 72 London LTNs implemented in 2020 finding ~49% casualty reduction; (f) **composite borough scorecards** (Healthy Streets Scorecard, weighted multi-indicator index) that convert raw datasets into a single rankable, shareable politician-facing number; and (g) **perception/survey barometers** (Fahrradklima-Test, Baromètre des Villes Cyclables) that substitute for hard infrastructure data where it's patchy, converting subjective rider sentiment into comparable city rankings that generate local press and political embarrassment/pride.

## 3. Documented wins/failures where data played a role

**Wins:** Space for Cycling's ward-by-ward specificity converted a vague "more cycling infrastructure" ask into 624 concrete, locally-owned demands, producing measurable candidate pledge rates. Waltham Forest's Mini-Holland/"Enjoy Waltham Forest" scheme is the most-cited UK data-vindicated success: independently evaluated by University of Westminster (self-reported activity increases) and King's College London's Environmental Research Group (air-quality/health modelling), producing citable "up to six weeks added life expectancy" claims used in subsequent advocacy elsewhere ([airqualitynews.com](https://airqualitynews.com/2018/08/03/waltham-forest-sees-health-benefit-from-cycling-and-walking-scheme/)). Seville is the international "rapid execution outperforms endless studies" precedent.

**Contested/failure terrain:** LTNs remain the most data-litigated UK street intervention — opponents and proponents both marshal count data, and even the pro-LTN evidence review documents real minority-of-sites traffic increases (26.5% of internal sites, 52.6% of boundary sites in the 2023 study), meaning the data itself is genuinely mixed, not one-sided — campaigners have had to learn to report *distributions*, not just averages, to keep credibility. The Waltham Forest air-quality modelling was itself publicly challenged as methodologically "frail" by a critical local blog, illustrating how contested even "good" evidence becomes once politicized ([walthamforestmatters.org.uk](https://www.walthamforestmatters.org.uk/2020/04/09/lbwf-mini-holland-and-air-quality-the-kings-college-environmental-research-group-report-and-its-frailties/)).

## 4. Implications for the persona

This world's actors are volunteer-driven, borough/ward-obsessed, allergic to vague citywide claims, and fluent in converting official government data (TfL, STATS19) into locally-addressable, politician-facing asks with dates and named streets attached. Their vocabulary: "ask," "pledge," "corridor," "before/after," "boundary road displacement," "mode share," "scorecard," "input vs. outcome indicator," "consultation," "safety in numbers." Their frustration is chronic: official data is real but frequently stale, city-wide-averaged, or too coarse to defend a single ward/ally's claim, forcing volunteers to hand-build crowdsourced supplements (Cyclescape issue logs, CycleStreets Photomap) to fill gaps — a strong signal that a Chicago tool's *ward-level danger index*, *per-alderman sponsorship tracking*, and *report-card grading of named routes* would land as exactly the missing infrastructure this persona already improvises by hand. Their latent, unstated need is less "more data" and more a *standing, always-current, locally-filterable index* that turns fragmented official datasets into a single comparable score per political geography — precisely what Healthy Streets Scorecard and Space for Cycling do manually and periodically, and what a live dashboard could do continuously.

## Data-need signals (analyst-extracted)

- A live, continuously-updated ward/alderman-level danger index would meet a need that London campaigners currently fill by manually re-running the Healthy Streets Scorecard on an annual cycle.
- Per-alderman sponsorship tracking on bike-safety legislation mirrors LCC's candidate-pledge tracking and would let users see which local politicians have and haven't committed, by name.
- Before/after count and casualty-rate views (not just single-point averages) are needed so users can see the full distribution of outcomes, since UK LTN data shows meaningful minority-case reversals that pure averages hide.
- A composite, weighted multi-indicator score per ward (inputs like protected-lane mileage plus outcomes like collision rate) would match the Healthy Streets Scorecard model campaigners already trust and cite to press and politicians.
- Route/corridor-level 'report card' grading (offstreet/protected/painted/none) matches how TfL's Strategic Cycling Analysis is used by LCC to prioritize and justify specific street-level asks.
- A crowdsourced hazard/obstruction layer (even mocked pending partnership) fills the same gap CycleStreets' Photomap and Cyclescape issue-tracking fill for UK campaigners when official data is too coarse or stale for a specific block.
