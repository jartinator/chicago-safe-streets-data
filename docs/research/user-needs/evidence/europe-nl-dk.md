# Evidence Brief: Dutch & Danish Bicycle Advocacy — Data Practices and What They Imply for a Persona

## 1. Who the real actors are and how they operate

**Fietsersbond** (Dutch Cyclists' Union) is a 32,000-member national NGO with 150 local branches and ~1,500 active volunteers, run from a 25-person Utrecht office ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fietsersbond); [Fietsersbond](https://www.fietsersbond.nl/english-info/more-about-fietsersbond/)). It began as street-activist descendants of the 1970s **Stop de Kindermoord** ("Stop the Child Murder") movement, which formed in 1973 after Dutch traffic deaths peaked at 3,264 in 1972 (450+ of them children) and organized occupations of crash "black spots," car-free play-street days, and a 15,000-person 1978 Amsterdam protest ([Streetsblog USA](https://usa.streetsblog.org/2013/02/20/the-origins-of-hollands-stop-murdering-children-street-safety-movement); [Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Transportation_Planning_Casebook/Stop_de_kindermoord)). Fietsersbond has since matured into a quasi-official advisory body that municipalities consult on cycling infrastructure design ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fietsersbond)).

**CROW** is the non-profit Dutch technical standards body (advising Rijkswaterstaat and the Ministry of Infrastructure) that publishes the **Design Manual for Bicycle Traffic**, the field's most influential planning manual, built around five design principles — cohesion, directness, safety, comfort, attractiveness — applied at the network level before individual projects are designed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CROW_Design_Manual_for_Bicycle_Traffic); [CROW Platform](https://crowplatform.com/product/design-manual-for-bicycle-traffic/)).

**Dutch Cycling Embassy (DCE)** is a government-established public-private network of 100+ Dutch organizations that exports Dutch cycling expertise abroad via study tours, masterclasses, and a member-only knowledge/toolkit platform ([DCE](https://dutchcycling.nl/about-us/our-organisation/)). **Tour de Force** is the national multi-stakeholder cooperation (governments, industry, NGOs, knowledge institutes) targeting 20% more cycled kilometers by 2027 vs. 2017, coordinated across 14 "ambitious cycling cities" ([Government.nl](https://www.government.nl/topics/bicycles/tour-de-force); [Bicycle Dutch](https://bicycledutch.wordpress.com/2020/01/02/2020-is-the-year-of-stage-2-of-the-tour-de-force/)).

**Cycling Embassy of Denmark (CED)**, founded 2009 at Velo-city Brussels, is a formal association of 40+ member organizations (companies, municipalities, NGOs) run out of the Danish Cyclists' Federation's secretariat, offering lectures, guided tours, and masterclasses to visiting delegations ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_Embassy_of_Denmark); [Cycling Solutions](https://cyclingsolutions.info/embassy/about-us-2/)).

**SWOV** (Institute for Road Safety Research, The Hague) is the authoritative Dutch crash-statistics body; its recent fact sheets show cyclists have been the largest fatality category for five-plus consecutive years (246 of 675 deaths in 2024; 281 in 2025) ([SWOV](https://swov.nl/en/fact-sheet/road-deaths-netherlands); [Global Cycling Safety](https://global-cycling-safety.org/en/2026/04/road-deaths-netherlands-2025-cyclists-highest-risk/)).

**BYPAD** (Bicycle Policy Audit, since 1999, EU-funded) is a certified audit instrument that convenes politicians, planners, and cyclist-user representatives to jointly score a municipality's cycling policy across 9 modules via a structured questionnaire, producing a public quality score, an action plan, and a certificate/label ([BYPAD/POLIS](https://www.polisnetwork.eu/project/bypad/); [TRIMIS](https://trimis.ec.europa.eu/project/bicycle-policy-audit)).

## 2. What data they actually use, publish, or demand

- **Copenhagen's Bicycle Account (Cykelregnskab)**: published biennially since 1996 (city cycling data reported since 1995), tracking budget for cycling infrastructure, network length, modal share, cyclist counts, accident statistics, and a **standardized biennial satisfaction survey** — the account's most-used component. The 2022 edition reported 80% satisfaction with cycle-track width, 55% with cycle-track quantity, 47% with parking, 68% with maintenance, against explicit 2025 targets (80%/80%) — i.e., KPIs with numeric political targets, not just descriptive stats ([Bicycle Account 2022 PDF](https://kk.sites.itera.dk/apps/kk_pub2/pdf/2420_d4db2492337f.pdf); [Cycling Embassy of Denmark](https://cyclingsolutions.info/the-national-bicycle-account-collects-data-on-cycling/)). Satisfaction rose from 83% (2006) to 97% (2016) per the 2016 account ([PDF](https://kk.sites.itera.dk/apps/kk_pub2/pdf/1698_21dbd0e48795.pdf)).
- **Permanent automatic bicycle counters**: Copenhagen deployed visible above-ground counters starting May 2009 (Rådhuspladsen) and June 2009 (Dronning Louises Bro), explicitly designed so cyclists could *see* the running tally — a data-as-morale-and-advocacy-tool device; 10 million riders passed one counter within its first year ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_Counter)).
- **Fietsersbond's Fietsbalans** (from 1999): a formal benchmarking methodology scoring Dutch towns on 11 objective criteria derived from CROW's five principles (directness, obstruction/comfort, surface, attractiveness, car-competitiveness, parking, bike-use rate, road safety, density, cyclist satisfaction, transport policy), combining official data, municipal-official surveys, and cyclist opinion polls — explicitly built so cities could benchmark against each other and "spring into action" on receiving low scores ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2023.1127742/full); [Fietsersbond](https://www.fietsersbond.nl/english-info/more-about-fietsersbond/)).
- **Fietsersbond Meldpunt** (crowdsourced hazard-reporting app/site, fietsersbond.verbeterdebuurt.nl): residents photograph and geotag potholes, broken push-buttons, overgrown vegetation, faded markings, blocked/flooded cycle paths, or illegally parked vehicles on cycle infrastructure; reports route to the responsible municipality ([Fietsersbond Meldpunt](https://fietsersbond.verbeterdebuurt.nl/); [Fietsersbond nieuws](https://www.fietsersbond.nl/nieuws/nieuw-meldpunt-met-app-is-fietsersbond-basic/)) — a direct structural analog to Bike Lane Uprising / 311 obstruction proxies.
- **SWOV crash statistics** feed directly into national safety targets and are cited by advocates as evidence rising e-bike use and cycling volumes correlate with rising *absolute* crash risk even as infrastructure improves — a nuance current Dutch advocates now grapple with publicly ([SWOV](https://swov.nl/en/fact-sheet/road-deaths-netherlands)).
- **BYPAD's 9-module scored questionnaire** functions as a structured, comparable audit instrument filled out by politicians, school directors, police traffic-safety officers, and cyclist-organization reps together — a governance-data hybrid, not just infrastructure counts ([POLIS](https://www.polisnetwork.eu/project/bypad/)).
- **Copenhagenize Index**: an independently run (now EIT Urban Mobility-backed) 14-criteria ranking of world cities, relaunched in 2025 after a 6-year gap, combining "data-driven" quantitative measures with qualitative/political-ambition scoring — Utrecht, Copenhagen, Amsterdam, and Ghent topped the 2025 edition ([EIT Urban Mobility](https://www.eiturbanmobility.eu/the-copenhagenize-index-2025-eit-urban-mobility-edition-reveals-the-best-bicycle-friendly-cities/); [Copenhagenize Index](https://copenhagenizeindex.eu/index.php/about/)).

## 3. Documented wins/failures where data played a role

- Stop de Kindermoord's child-fatality counts directly reframed 1970s Dutch road policy, contributing to an eventual 83% drop in traffic deaths from the 1972 peak to a 2013/2014 low ([Streetsblog](https://usa.streetsblog.org/2013/02/20/the-origins-of-hollands-stop-murdering-children-street-safety-movement)).
- Fietsbalans scorecards are credited with prompting concrete municipal action — cities "sprang into action" on receiving unfavorable published comparisons ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/future-transportation/articles/10.3389/ffutr.2023.1127742/full)).
- In 's-Hertogenbosch, a resident/green-party coalition citing "100+ crashes since 2014, 3 deaths" in a specific district drove the municipality to redesign two intersections for cyclist/pedestrian safety — a clean case of localized crash-count data driving a project-level fix ([Bicycle Dutch](https://bicycledutch.wordpress.com/2021/02/03/two-intersections-improved-for-cycling/)).
- Current tension/failure signal: despite infrastructure investment, SWOV data shows cyclist fatalities rising for 5-6 consecutive years and now exceeding car-occupant deaths — advocates and researchers (e.g., SWOV's Teun Uitdewilligen) are using this to argue exposure/volume growth is outpacing safety gains, a genuinely contested, current data debate rather than an unqualified success story ([SWOV](https://swov.nl/en/fact-sheet/road-deaths-netherlands); [ETSC](https://etsc.eu/dutch-road-safety-thrown-back-in-time-15-years/)).

## 4. Implications for the persona

This world's practitioners are fluent in **network-level thinking** (CROW's cohesion/directness/safety/comfort/attractiveness) and impatient with **project-by-project, intersection-by-intersection** US planning that lacks a named grid standard. Their vocabulary includes "modal share," "cyclist satisfaction index," "black spot," "benchmarking," "quality audit," and biennial reporting cadence as a *political ritual*, not just a technical exercise. They expect KPIs to have explicit numeric targets tied to years (Copenhagen's 80%-by-2025), not vague aspirational language. They expect crash data to be independently, centrally maintained (SWOV) rather than assembled ad hoc by advocates. They are used to obstruction/hazard reporting apps that route directly to a responsible government office with tracked resolution, not just a public map. A latent, unstated need this surfaces for a Chicago tool: **cross-city or cross-ward benchmarking with a published, defensible scoring methodology** (Fietsbalans/BYPAD-style), a **satisfaction survey time series**, and transparency about where speculative/mock data (like the obstruction layer) sits relative to a "gold standard" verified layer — because this persona's whole professional instinct is to distrust unverified or proxy data and demand to know its provenance and limitations before using it to make claims.

## Data-need signals (analyst-extracted)

- Chicago's dashboard should publish explicit numeric year-bound targets for its danger index or safety KPIs (e.g., 'X% reduction by 2028'), not just raw counts, mirroring Copenhagen Bicycle Account-style politically-legible targets.
- A cross-ward or cross-city benchmarking score (Fietsbalans/BYPAD-style, built from named sub-criteria) would let this persona compare wards or peer cities on a defensible, published methodology rather than a single opaque danger index.
- The dashboard needs a visible, time-series cyclist-satisfaction or perceived-safety survey layer (not just crash/complaint counts) since Danish practice treats subjective satisfaction as a core biennial KPI alongside hard infrastructure metrics.
- Given SWOV's practice of centralizing authoritative crash data, this persona will want clear provenance/authority labeling on the crash dataset (who collects it, how verified) and clear separation from proxy/mock layers like the bike-lane-obstruction data.
- The mock bike-lane-obstruction layer needs explicit, prominent 'unverified/pending partnership' labeling and a stated path to ground-truth verification, since this persona's professional norm (Meldpunt) is crowdsourced reports that route to and get resolved by a named responsible authority.
- A network-level (not just project/intersection-level) view of the CDOT bikeway network — showing cohesion/directness/connectivity gaps against a stated grid-spacing standard — would match this persona's CROW-trained instinct to evaluate networks, not isolated segments.
- Legislation/sponsorship tracking should be pairable with before/after crash or ridership metrics per ward, since this persona expects data to be used to hold specific officials accountable for measurable outcomes (as Fietsbalans and BYPAD do for municipalities).
