# Shared semi-structured interview guide

Used by the Lead Researcher with every persona. Sections run in order;
questions are starting points — follow-ups should chase specifics. The
stimulus (section 3) must come **after** sections 1–2 so open answers are not
anchored by what OYL already has.

## 1. Context (who they are when they touch data)

- Describe your role and the last decision you influenced (or tried to)
  about street safety. What did that actually involve, step by step?
- Who do you need to convince, and what does "convinced" look like for them?
- What information sources do you touch in a normal week for this work?

## 2. Current practice & workarounds

- Tell me about the last time you needed a number, map, or chart about bike
  safety. Where did you go? What did you do with it? How long did it take?
- What have you built yourself — spreadsheets, saved searches, screenshots,
  FOIA requests, hand counts — because nothing gave it to you directly?
- Tell me about a time data (or the lack of it) sank an argument you were
  making. What was missing?
- What data do you distrust, and what earned that distrust?

## 3. Stimulus walkthrough

Present the OYL inventory (`02-data-inventory.md`) in plain language, one
area at a time: the two maps, findings cards, ward table + CSV, the
council/alderman accountability data, hearings, menu money, the badge system
(real/proxy/mock), and the stated limitations (no ridership normalization,
mock obstructions, dooring undercount, no install dates).

For each area, capture the immediate reaction before explaining further:
- What would you use this for, concretely — in what meeting, document, or
  conversation?
- What would you need to check before you'd trust it in front of your
  audience?

## 4. Gap probing

- You're preparing for [persona-appropriate scenario: a council hearing / a
  ward night / a campaign launch / choosing a route for your kid]. Open OYL.
  What do you look for first? Where does it fail you?
- Of everything OYL does NOT have — what single absence costs you most?
- If OYL handed you one export/artifact each week, what would it contain,
  and who would you forward it to?
- The site refuses to normalize by ridership because it has no volume data.
  Does that caveat change how you'd use it? What would you accept as
  good-enough exposure data?

## 5. Magic-wand close

- Magic wand: one dataset that doesn't publicly exist appears, clean and
  current. What is it?
- One chart or map you could put on a screen in front of your hardest
  audience that ends an argument. Describe it.
- What would make you *stop* using a site like this after trying it once?

## Persona-scenario substitutions (section 4)

| Persona class | Scenario |
|---|---|
| European strategist / think-tank analyst | advising a city on network targets |
| Ward campaigner / Chicago advocate | building a ward-level campaign ask |
| Government insider | briefing an alderman before a vote / menu deadline |
| Everyday resident / parent | deciding a route; deciding whether to email the alderman |
| Community organizer | a community meeting where residents doubt official data |
