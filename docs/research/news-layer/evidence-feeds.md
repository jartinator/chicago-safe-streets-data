# Evidence Brief: News-Coverage Layer Feasibility (RSS/feed inputs for a weekly, deterministic pipeline)

*Method: live WebFetch verification of candidate feed URLs (not just search-snippet inference) against chi.streetsblog.org, blockclubchicago.org, chicago.suntimes.com, chicagotribune.com, news.wttw.com, thedailyline.com, activetransportation.org, and news.google.com/rss, plus WebSearch for licensing/ToS language and robots.txt fetches, July 13 2026. Every "valid/N items/titles" claim below reflects an actual fetch in this session, not a cached assumption.*

## 1. Feed availability per outlet (live-verified)

**Streetsblog Chicago (chi.streetsblog.org) — WordPress, fully fetchable, best-fit source.**
`https://chi.streetsblog.org/feed/` returned valid RSS 2.0, 10 items, with `<title>`, `<link>`, `<dc:creator>`, `<pubDate>`, multiple `<category>` tags, `<guid>`, mostly-empty `<description>`, and full article HTML in `<content:encoded>` — i.e. **full text is in the feed**, not just an excerpt. Sample items and their `<category>` tags: "CTA board approved deal with IDOT to fix Blue Line slow zones…" (CTA, Blue Line, Bus Lane Enforcement…); "This year People for Bikes' City Ratings ranked Chicago…" (Complete Streets, City Ratings, Erica Schroeder, People for Bikes, Promoted). Two of the ten items in this pull were "Today's Headlines for [day]" daily link-roundup posts, not original reporting — a pipeline should filter these out by title pattern.

Streetsblog also runs **per-tag feeds that are exactly the taxonomy this module needs**, and all three tested resolved and returned real content:
- `https://chi.streetsblog.org/tag/milwaukee-avenue/feed/` → 6 items, all specifically about Milwaukee Ave bike lanes (e.g. "Eyes on the street: Taking a spin on the new-and-improved Milwaukee protected bike lanes in River West…").
- `https://chi.streetsblog.org/tag/augusta-boulevard/feed/` → 4 items, all Augusta Blvd-specific.
- `https://chi.streetsblog.org/tag/pat-dowell/feed/` → 9 items, all about Ald. Pat Dowell (3rd Ward), including "Once Again, Alderman Dowell Tries to Block a Sustainable Transportation Amenity" and "The Tribune Praises Pat Dowell's 'Expertise in Urban Planning'" — confirming Streetsblog maintains **per-alderman tag pages/feeds** as well as per-street.

By contrast, the narrower topical sub-category feed `https://chi.streetsblog.org/category/complete-streets/bicycling/feed/` is valid RSS but **sparse and stale** — 8 items spanning May 2023–May 2026 with month-plus gaps — meaning that specific "bicycling" category is not consistently applied to bike-relevant stories and cannot be relied on alone; the main firehose plus keyword/tag matching is the better strategy. `robots.txt` at `chi.streetsblog.org/robots.txt` is fully permissive (`User-agent: *` / `Disallow:` blank, plus sitemap links) — no AI-bot exclusions, no path restrictions.

**Block Club Chicago (blockclubchicago.org) — WordPress/Newspack, root feed works, category/tag feeds got rate-limited (429) in this session.**
`https://blockclubchicago.org/feed/` returned valid RSS 2.0 twice in this session (6–10 items each time), with `<title>`, `<link>`, `<dc:creator>`, `<pubDate>`, `<category>` (multiple, including neighborhood names like "Uptown/Edgewater/Rogers Park" alongside topic tags like "Transportation" and "Housing"), `<description>` (a genuine 1–3 sentence excerpt, not empty), `<content:encoded>` (full article text), `<guid>`, and a WordPress `<post-id>`. One item in the July 13 pull, "'Moving Archer Forward' Initiative Aims To Bring 800 New Housing Units To Archer Avenue," carried the category `Transportation` — confirming Block Club **does** tag posts with a topical "Transportation" category alongside neighborhood categories, i.e. filtering could in principle happen server-side via a category feed.

However, every attempt to fetch a **category or tag feed** specifically — `category/transportation/feed/`, `tag/bike-lanes/feed/`, `tag/bike-lanes/?feed=rss2` — returned **HTTP 429 Too Many Requests**, while the root `/feed/` kept succeeding. WebSearch confirms the tag archive pages exist as human-facing pages (`blockclubchicago.org/tag/bike-lanes/`, `blockclubchicago.org/tag/bike-lane/`) but their machine-readable feed endpoints could not be verified live in this session. This is plausibly bot-detection specific to this fetcher (see §4 robots.txt finding — Block Club explicitly names and disallows `ClaudeBot` and other AI crawlers site-wide, even though there is no blanket `Disallow: /` for unnamed user agents), not necessarily how a distinctly-identified, low-frequency weekly cron job would fare. **Flagged as unverified — retest with a plain-HTTP client outside this tool before relying on it.**

**Chicago Sun-Times (chicago.suntimes.com) — root feed works, no verified topic-specific feed.**
`https://chicago.suntimes.com/feed` returned a valid feed (the analysis tool read it as Atom-structured; either way it parsed as valid XML) with 10 items — general Chicago news (crime, health, entertainment), **none transportation-specific in the sampled window**, and no visible per-item category field in the fields the fetch reported. `https://chicago.suntimes.com/arc/outboundfeeds/rss/` (a guess at an Arc Publishing outbound-feed pattern) and `https://chicago.suntimes.com/rss/` (an index page guess) both returned **404**. No transportation-specific Sun-Times RSS URL was found or confirmed working; a feed-directory search (Feedspot) suggested a `chicago.suntimes.com/rss/tra...` pattern exists but the full URL was truncated in search results and was not independently verified by fetch — **unconfirmed**.

**Chicago Tribune (chicagotribune.com) — blocked at the tool level, unconfirmed.**
Every WebFetch attempt (`chicagotribune.com/feed/`, `www.chicagotribune.com/feed/`) returned a hard tool-level error — "Claude Code is unable to fetch from chicagotribune.com" — before any HTTP request/response could even be reported. This looks like a domain-level restriction in this fetch tool (likely tied to Tribune's known paywall/anti-bot posture) rather than a live 403/429 from the server, so it's **inconclusive** whether a normal script could reach a Tribune feed. Given Tribune content already surfaced through the Google News query (see §2) via aggregation, Tribune is likely reachable that way even if direct-feed access is unverified.

**WTTW News (news.wttw.com) — root feed works, low bike-relevance density.**
`https://news.wttw.com/feed` returned valid RSS, 12 items, fields title/link/pubDate/category, categories including "Crime & Law," "Politics," "Science & Nature," and a recurring "Week in Review" segment. **None of the 12 sampled items were about bikes or transportation** — this is a general-assignment public-TV newsroom feed, and its actual transportation-and-politics reporter (Nick Blumberg, per WTTW's own bio copy) produces low-frequency, high-value pieces (e.g. the CDOT bike-count workaround story cited in `docs/foia/cdot-counter-crawl.md`) that will mostly need keyword filtering against the general feed. Two guessed topical paths, `news.wttw.com/politics/feed` (403) and `news.wttw.com/rss.xml` (returned the wrong feed — WTTW's "Daily Chicagoan" newsletter digest, not News articles), did not pan out; no verified WTTW transportation-specific feed exists.

**The Daily Line (thedailyline.com) — no RSS, paywalled.**
No `<link rel="alternate" type="application/rss+xml">` was found on the homepage, no visible RSS icon, and the site is **subscription-gated** ("You do not have access to this content. Upgrade to TDL Professional or add a TDL Chicago membership to unlock all Chicago content" — tiers $395–$695/year). A handful of articles are marked "UNLOCKED" but most City Hall/committee coverage — exactly the meeting/vote material this module wants — sits behind the paywall. **The Daily Line is not viable as an automated feed source**; note also that `thedailyline.net` (a domain the task brief suggested) is not the outlet's real domain — it resolves to an unrelated site with a mismatched TLS certificate.

**Active Transportation Alliance blog (activetransportation.org) — feed exists, blocked in this session.**
The org's domain has moved from `activetrans.org` to `activetransportation.org` (301 redirect confirmed live). `https://activetransportation.org/blog/feed` returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden** in this session. Feedspot's directory independently lists `activetrans.org/blog/feed` (redirecting) and `activetrans.org/feed` as known feed URLs, so the feed almost certainly exists — this looks like the same bot-detection pattern seen on Block Club's category feeds rather than a genuinely absent feed. **Flagged as unverified — retest with a distinctly-identified, low-frequency client.**

## 2. Google News RSS as aggregator fallback

`https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=%22bike+lane%22+Chicago+when:7d&hl=en-US&gl=US&ceid=US:en` returned valid RSS with 10–11 items: title, `link` (a `news.google.com/rss/articles/[opaque-id]` **redirect URL, not the direct publisher URL**), `pubDate`, `guid`, `description`, and a `source url=` attribute naming the outlet. In one query pull it surfaced: two *Chicago Tribune* opinion letters ("Aldermanic prerogative stands in the way of a better bike lane network in Chicago"; "Here's why I am opposed to segregated bike lanes in Chicago"), Streetsblog Chicago's PeopleForBikes-ranking story and a "Today's Headlines" digest, a *McKinley Park News* piece on West 35th St protected bike lanes, plus CTA/CBS/ABC7/Yahoo items of varying relevance — a genuinely useful cross-outlet mix, and notably it **is a working path to Tribune content even though direct Tribune-feed access is unverified/blocked**.

Link redirection is real and non-trivial: per WebSearch findings, Google now wraps article links in an encoded payload; the historical trick of base64-decoding the URL segment no longer reliably works because Google added a signature/timestamp step requiring a request to Google's internal `batchexecute` endpoint to resolve the true source URL (cloro.dev; n8n community thread; a Hex.pm `GoogleNewsDecoder` library exists for this). **The simpler, robust option for a weekly cron is to just follow the HTTP redirect** (issue a GET/HEAD on the `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` URL and read the resulting `Location`/final URL) rather than decode the payload — this works without reverse-engineering Google's internal API, though it costs one extra HTTP round-trip per item.

On ToS/reliability: this is an **undocumented, unsupported endpoint** — "not a supported product with a contract, no status page, no version header, no deprecation notice" — that Google can change or throttle without notice (cloro.dev). A July-2026 sampling cited in that source found item recency skewed old (median item age ~6.6 days, only 7.6% under 6 hours old), which is actually *fine* for a **weekly** cron cadence but would be a problem for anything more real-time. Aggressive/high-volume scraping of Google News search results (as opposed to the documented-adjacent RSS search endpoint used sparingly) risks CAPTCHA challenges and is explicitly against Google's general ToS for automated querying; a once-a-week, few-queries-per-run pull is a very different risk profile than what scraping guides warn about and is very unlikely to trigger enforcement, but there is no formal guarantee.

## 3. Tag/category taxonomy usefulness

**Streetsblog Chicago is the clear winner here.** It maintains individually addressable, live, WordPress-native tag feeds per street/corridor (`tag/milwaukee-avenue/feed/`, `tag/augusta-boulevard/feed/` — both verified) and per named official (`tag/pat-dowell/feed/` — verified, 9/9 items on-topic). This means corridor- and alderman-level filtering can happen **server-side by feed choice** for Streetsblog, which is far more reliable than client-side keyword matching against a firehose. The catch: coverage is uneven — a given corridor or alderman only has a tag feed (with content) if Streetsblog has written about them enough; a first-pass build should confirm tag-slug existence per corridor of interest rather than assuming a URL pattern always resolves (several plausible tag slugs will 404).

**Block Club Chicago** tags by both **neighborhood** (dozens of neighborhood/area categories, e.g. "Uptown/Edgewater/Rogers Park," "Bridgeport/Chinatown/McKinley Park") and **topic** (a "Transportation" category was observed live on an Archer Ave article), so the taxonomy exists in principle for both geographic and topical server-side filtering. But because the category/tag *feed endpoints* returned 429 in every attempt this session (see §1), this could not be confirmed working end-to-end — **treat as "probably real, verify before building against it."** If category feeds prove unreachable in production too, fall back to client-side keyword + neighborhood-category matching against the always-working root `/feed/`.

Neither Sun-Times nor WTTW showed usable topic/place-specific feeds in this session — both would require client-side keyword filtering of their general firehoses.

## 4. Legal/licensing

**Verdict, in two sentences:** Republishing bare headline + link + publication date + source name — no article body, no images — from any of these outlets' RSS feeds is safe for a non-commercial open-source dashboard under settled US copyright doctrine, because facts and short factual titles are not copyrightable subject matter and linking to a public URL is not infringement (*Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co.*, 499 U.S. 340 (1991), holding facts/compilations of fact are not original authorship — law.cornell.edu). Block Club Chicago's own explicit republishing terms are more permissive than what OYL needs: their standard license lets partners republish **full article text** under **Creative Commons Attribution-NoDerivatives 4.0 International**, with restrictions aimed at wholesale/automatic bulk-republishing and resale/syndication (e.g. to Apple News or Google News) — restrictions that target full-text reuse, not the headline+link+date pattern.

Two things worth flagging, not as legal risk but as a stated-preference/norms issue: (a) both **Block Club Chicago** and the **Chicago Sun-Times** publish `robots.txt` files that explicitly name and disallow AI/LLM crawlers site-wide — `ClaudeBot`, `Claude-User`, `Claude-SearchBot`, `GPTBot`, `CCBot`, `Google-Extended`, `PerplexityBot`, etc. — while leaving no catch-all `Disallow: /` for unnamed/generic user agents (confirmed by direct robots.txt fetch, this session). This is a meaningful signal that these two publishers do not want *AI-branded* crawlers touching their sites, even though it does not technically forbid a plainly-labeled, low-frequency RSS-reader script with a distinct, honest User-Agent string making a weekly request to a public feed URL. A conservative, respectful build should (i) identify the pipeline's fetcher with its own descriptive, non-AI-branded User-Agent (e.g. `OnYourLeftNewsBot/1.0 (+https://... ; contact@...)`), (ii) fetch each feed at most once per scheduled run (weekly), and (iii) treat a 403/429/robots-block as "this outlet opts out, skip it and rely on Google News for their headlines instead" rather than working around the block. (b) No dedicated Terms-of-Service or FAQ page for either Streetsblog Chicago or Block Club Chicago was found to contain an explicit RSS-reuse clause distinct from their full-article republishing terms — the headline+link+date pattern falls outside what either site's policies were written to address, which is consistent with it being uncontroversial (nobody writes a policy for a use nobody would object to) but means there's no explicit written blessing either.

## 5. Matching feasibility — honest assessment

**What matches deterministically, well:** Street/corridor names in headlines and Streetsblog's own tag taxonomy — confirmed directly (Milwaukee Avenue, Augusta Boulevard tag feeds, both 100% on-topic). Alderman surnames also matched cleanly in the `pat-dowell` tag feed (9/9 items about Dowell), and appeared unprompted in general-feed and Google News headlines too (e.g. "The Tribune Praises Pat Dowell's 'Expertise in Urban Planning,'" "Which alders voted against the potentially lifesaving ordinance" style headlines cited in the existing `docs/research/user-needs/evidence/chicago-system.md` brief). A keyword/regex pass over street names (from OYL's own corridor list) and alderman surnames (from the 50-ward roster) against headline + first paragraph should catch a large share of relevant items.

**What does not match, essentially ever:** Council record numbers (e.g. `O2026-0026797`) **do not appear in any headline or excerpt observed in this session**, across ~50 distinct titles pulled from Streetsblog, Block Club, WTTW, Sun-Times, and Google News. This matches the task's own prior — record numbers are internal Council/eLMS bookkeeping identifiers that journalists never put in a headline or lede, and general-audience news copy almost never mentions them anywhere in the body either (reporters write "an ordinance introduced by Ald. X" not the record number). **Do not build a matcher that expects record numbers to appear in news text.** The realistic path to connect a news item to a specific record number is the reverse: match the news item to an alderman + rough topic + date window, then cross-reference the ward's known open legislative items via the eLMS matter API (already documented in the repo's `elms-matter-api-and-agenda-pdfs.md` memory) for a plausible record-number candidate — not a direct text match.

**What's in between:** Committee/meeting references are inconsistent — some headlines explicitly name a committee ("Green light for the Red Line extension: TIF passes in committee, heads to full Council") while most don't; matching a news item to a specific *upcoming* meeting/vote will mostly depend on date proximity plus alderman/topic overlap rather than text extraction.

## 6. Volume estimate

From live-observed feed contents (not archive-page guessing): **Streetsblog Chicago's main feed** produced roughly 5 substantive (non-"Today's Headlines") posts over a 6-day window (Jul 7–13, 2026), of which 2 were squarely bike-safety/bike-network relevant (the PeopleForBikes City Ratings response, the Friends of the Crosstown Trail piece) and the rest were adjacent transit/CTA items — call it **roughly 2–3 core bike-safety items/week** from Streetsblog alone, since it's a transportation-focused outlet with a high relevance density. **Block Club Chicago** is a much higher-volume, general-assignment outlet (6 posts observed in a single morning across all topics/neighborhoods) where bike/transportation-specific posts are a small fraction of a large firehose — the one "Transportation"-tagged item seen in this sample was about housing near a corridor, not safety per se; based on the tag-archive search results (a spread of Block Club bike-lane stories roughly every 2–6 weeks historically, spiking around specific news events like the 2026 CDOT economic-impact study), a realistic estimate is **roughly 1 bike/street-safety item/week from Block Club**, unevenly distributed (some weeks zero, some weeks several around a news event). WTTW and Sun-Times contribute more sporadically — maybe **0–1 item/week combined** based on the general-firehose samples pulled (no bike-specific items appeared in either 10–12-item sample). Google News aggregation adds redundant coverage of the same underlying stories plus occasional outlets not otherwise covered (Tribune letters, suburban outlets like McKinley Park News).

**Rough total: 4–6 genuinely new bike/street-safety Chicago items per week** across all viable feeds in a typical week, with spikes to 10+ around a major news event (an ordinance vote, a crash, a CDOT report release) and quiet weeks with 1–2. This is a workable volume for a weekly digest module — small enough to review/tag by hand if the deterministic matcher misses something, large enough to be worth automating.

Same bot-detection caveat as above — **needs retesting outside this tool.**
- **Chicago Tribune feed access** — WebFetch refused to even attempt the request ("Claude Code is unable to fetch from chicagotribune.com"), a tool-level restriction rather than a live server response. Tribune's actual RSS availability and any 403/paywall behavior toward automated clients is **completely unconfirmed** by this research; Tribune content is reachable via Google News aggregation regardless.
- **Chicago Sun-Times topic-specific (e.g. transportation) feed URL** — a Feedspot search snippet implied a URL pattern like `chicago.suntimes.com/rss/tra...` exists but the full URL was truncated and never independently confirmed; both guessed alternatives (`/arc/outboundfeeds/rss/`, `/rss/`) 404'd. Only the general firehose (`/feed`) is confirmed working.
- **WTTW News topic-specific feed** — no working transportation- or politics-scoped feed URL was found; `/politics/feed` 403'd and `/rss.xml` turned out to be an unrelated newsletter digest feed, not News articles. Only the general `/feed` firehose is confirmed.
- **Whether Sun-Times' `<description>` field is full text or excerpt** — the fetch tool's summary did not clearly characterize this field; unconfirmed.
- **Formal legal sign-off** — none of this is a substitute for actual legal review; the Feist/facts-not-copyrightable analysis and the read of Block Club's CC BY-ND terms represent a good-faith non-lawyer assessment of publicly available policy text and case law, not legal advice. If OYL ever moves beyond headline+link+date (e.g. quoting a sentence of body text), that changes the analysis and should get a real look.
- **Google's actual current tolerance for even low-volume News RSS querying** — no official Google statement was found (the endpoint is explicitly unsupported/undocumented); the "should be fine at weekly-cron volume" read is an inference from third-party scraping-guide commentary, not a source-confirmed ToS position.

## Overall feasibility call: **YELLOW — viable with fallbacks, not a clean green**

Streetsblog Chicago alone is strong enough (permissive robots.txt, full-text feed, working street- and alderman-level tag feeds, no paywall) to justify building the module; it should be the primary, always-on source. Google News RSS is a solid low-effort fallback/supplement for Tribune, Sun-Times, and outlets Streetsblog doesn't cover, with the caveat that it's an unsupported endpoint and links need redirect-following. Block Club Chicago and Active Transportation Alliance are probably fine for a real weekly-cadence, honestly-labeled client, but that could not be proven in this session (429/403) and must be retested outside this tool before the pipeline design leans on their category/tag feeds specifically. The Daily Line is a dead end (paywalled, no feed) — anything from committee/City-Hall-insider coverage will have to come from Streetsblog/Block Club/WTTW's own committee reporting instead. Record-number matching will never work from news text and the design should not attempt it; street/corridor and alderman-surname matching is genuinely strong and should carry most of the linking logic.
