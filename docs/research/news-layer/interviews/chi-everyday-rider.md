# Interview: chi-everyday-rider — news-coverage concept (2026-07-13)

*Persona: Jess Morales, 34, nurse in Avondale. Composite/fictional, per
`docs/research/user-needs/personas/chi-everyday-rider.md`.*

---

**Interviewer:** Walk me through the last time a news story about biking or
streets in Chicago changed something you did — what was the story, how did
it reach you, and what happened next?

**Jess:** Okay, so — this was maybe six weeks ago. Somebody in the Avondale
Neighbors group posted a Block Club link, "driver strikes cyclist near
Belmont and Kimball," something like that headline. I didn't click it right
away, I was at work. But three different people I actually know commented
under it — one of them said it was right by the Jewel, which is basically on
our route to school some mornings when we skip the trailer path. That's what
got me. Not the headline, the comment saying "that's right by the Jewel."

So that week I just... didn't take Kimball. Went two blocks out of the way
on the quieter street even though it's longer, because now I had this
picture in my head every time I got near that intersection. Did that for
like ten days, then went back to normal because nothing else happened and
you can't live your whole life rerouting off one story. But yeah — it wasn't
the article itself, it was a neighbor telling me where it was in words I
already understood.

---

**Interviewer:** When something bike-related happens in your neighborhood —
a crash, a new lane, a fight about parking — how do you usually find out
today? What's frustrating about that?

**Jess:** Facebook group, mostly. Avondale Neighbors, and there's a
Logan Square one I lurk in too since it bleeds into our area. Block Club
Chicago app sends me a push notification sometimes, if it's a big enough
story. And honestly? My kid's school pickup line. Another mom told me
before either of the other two that they were putting some kind of concrete
thing — I think it's called a bump-out? — on the corner by school, because
her husband saw the crew out there.

What's frustrating is nobody agrees on what actually happened or what it
means. One post says the alderman is "finally doing something," the reply
under it says he's been promising that same lane for two years and this is
an election-year photo op. I don't know who's right. And there's no one
place that just tells me straight — is my actual route different now, yes
or no. I end up piecing it together from three sources that don't talk to
each other.

---

**Interviewer:** *[reads the stimulus]* React honestly. What here would you
actually use, what would you ignore, what would you distrust?

**Jess:** Okay, "derived" — see, that word right there, you lost me for a
second. I had to read it twice. Is that like a guess? Just say it's a guess.

But setting that aside — the actual idea, headlines from Streetsblog and
Block Club sitting on my ward page — I mean, sure, I'd glance at it. I
already read half of those anyway from the Facebook group, so having them
in one spot instead of scrolling through forty comments to find the actual
link, that's not nothing. If I'm already on the site checking my street, and
there's a real headline sitting right there instead of me going and digging
for it, fine, I'll read the headline.

What I'd ignore: the alderman-name matching part. I don't actually know
what ward I'm in, I know my alderman's name because his face is on a
banner near my building, but the ward number means nothing to me. So if the
whole system is built on "ward number, alderman name, street name," that's
fine on your end, doesn't change what I do with it.

What I'd distrust — honestly, the fact that you're telling me upfront it
"can occasionally be wrong." That's an odd thing to lead with. Like, why
would I trust the headline is about my actual corner if you're telling me
the computer guessed? I'd probably still click it, but I'd have that little
seed of "eh, might not even be my street" in my head before I even read it.

---

**Interviewer:** Imagine you're on your ward's page and see three
headlines there — two from Streetsblog, one from Block Club. Does the
dashboard showing you these change how you see the dashboard itself? Would
it feel like the dashboard is taking sides?

**Jess:** Huh. Yeah, actually — a little. I don't really know what
Streetsblog is, if I'm honest. Block Club, I read that, that's just news,
that's the same place that tells me about the taqueria closing or the water
main. But if I saw two headlines from some outlet I've never heard of and
one from the one I trust, I'd wonder who Streetsblog even is and whether
they're, like, a bike people group. Not in a bad way necessarily, but it'd
make me go "oh, is this whole dashboard secretly a bike-advocacy thing
dressed up as a data site?" I wasn't sure about that already, honestly, and
that would tip me a little further toward "yeah, this is for the bike
people, not really for me."

That's probably not fair to you, but that's genuinely what would go through
my head in the three seconds I'm looking at it before I move on to check my
actual street.

---

**Interviewer:** Now suppose one of those three headlines turns out to be
about a street in a different ward — matched by mistake. You clicked it and
figured that out. What does that do for you?

**Jess:** Honestly? I'd just quietly stop clicking those. I wouldn't be
furious, it's not like the pothole that took four months to fix, I wouldn't
go complain about it anywhere. But the next time I saw that little news box
on the page, I'd basically scroll past it the way I scroll past ads now. My
brain would file it as "that part's not reliable, skip it" — and the thing
is, I probably wouldn't separate that out from the rest of the site. Like,
if the news box got it wrong, some small part of me starts wondering if the
crash numbers are also a little off. That's probably not logical, I know
those are different things on your end, but from where I'm sitting it's all
just "the app told me something and it was wrong."

---

**Interviewer:** The feature deliberately does NOT tell you which news
story goes with which upcoming meeting or ordinance. Would you want it to
try, even if it were sometimes wrong?

**Jess:** No, actually — and this is one of those things where I don't
think I even care either way, if I'm being honest with you. I don't go to
the meetings. I've never been to a ward night in my life, I wouldn't know an
ordinance if it bit me. So whether a headline is "about" some meeting on the
calendar doesn't do anything for me, because the meeting itself doesn't do
anything for me. I'm not going to suddenly show up because a headline and a
meeting date are sitting near each other on a page.

If anything, don't bother — that sounds like more guessing on top of the
first guess, and you just told me the first guess is sometimes wrong.
Stacking a second maybe on top of a maybe, no thank you.

---

**Interviewer:** Magic wand — one thing about news plus this dashboard
you'd change or add?

**Jess:** I don't want a list of headlines, honestly, that's the part that
doesn't grab me at all — it's basically what my Facebook group already does,
just tidier. What I'd actually want is way smaller: if something in the
news is about my exact route — Kimball, the school street, wherever — just
tell me that, loud, at the top, not buried in a box of five headlines I have
to read to figure out if it matters. Like a "heads up" for my street,
not a newsfeed for my ward. I've got maybe forty-five seconds on this site
before my kid needs a snack or I need to clock in. Don't make me read
headlines to find out if my forty-five seconds matters — tell me up front.
