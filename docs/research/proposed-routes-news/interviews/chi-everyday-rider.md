# Interview: chi-everyday-rider — proposed-projects concept (2026-07-13)

*Persona: Jess Morales, 34, nurse in Avondale. Composite/fictional, per
`docs/research/user-needs/personas/chi-everyday-rider.md`.*

---

**Interviewer:** Have you ever heard about a bike project coming to your
area — a new lane, a trail, anything? Walk me through how you heard, what
you believed, and what actually happened.

**Jess:** Yeah — the 606 thing, the extension part. This was maybe two
years ago now. Block Club ran something like "606 extension could finally
cross the Kennedy," and I remember getting kind of excited, because that
would actually matter to me — I'm right there, if that trail pushed west
under the expressway I could maybe get Mateo to school without touching a
single car street at all. I told Danny that night, like "they're finally
extending the trail, it's happening," full stop, like it was basically
built already in my head.

And then... nothing. For like a year I didn't hear anything else about it.
I actually forgot about it until Priya mentioned something at pickup this
spring — "did you hear they got some of the money for the 606 extension" —
and I was like, wait, some of the money? I thought that was already
happening. Turns out no, it's still "in design" or whatever, two years
later, and I'd basically been telling people for a year it was a done deal
because of one headline I read once and never checked again. So — that's
the story. I heard it, I believed the most finished version of it in my
head, and the actual thing is still just paper somewhere.

---

**Interviewer:** When you want to know if a street near you is ever
getting fixed, what do you do today? What happens when you try?

**Jess:** Honestly, mostly nothing, because I don't really "do" anything —
I ask around. Like if I'm wondering about the corner by Mateo's school, I'll
ask another mom at pickup, or I'll post in Avondale Neighbors, "does anyone
know if they're doing anything about the bump-out by the school," and I'll
get like six different answers, half of them confident and wrong. One time
I did try to actually look it up — I think I googled "CDOT bike lane
Elston Avenue" — and I landed on some city page that was basically a list
of PDFs with names like "FY24 Bikeways Program" and I opened one and it was
a map of the whole city with tiny street names I couldn't even zoom in on
my phone. I closed it in under a minute. I've never called my alderman's
office about it either, even though I probably should — I don't even know
what I'd say, "is this happening," and then what, they read me a status off
a spreadsheet? So mostly I just ask a person, and the honest answer is I
never really find out until I either see it happen or somebody posts a
photo of the crew doing it.

---

**Interviewer:** *[reads the stimulus]* React honestly — what would you
use, ignore, distrust?

**Jess:** Okay, a few things at once here. The stuff I'd actually use —
honestly, the plain sentence and the status, if it's in words like that.
"In design," "funded in part," "installed, being modified" — that's the
first time anybody's described this stuff to me in a way I don't have to
translate. Compare that to the CDOT PDF I told you about, where I couldn't
even find the words "Elston Avenue" on the page. If it just said "606
extension — Kennedy crossing — in design, last checked June" I'd actually
believe I understood what I just read, first try.

The headlines — yeah, I mean, we talked about this before, right? I still
don't think I need a little box of three headlines, that's basically my
Facebook group, tidier. But I'll take it if it's already sitting there
while I'm reading the status anyway, sure, I'd glance at the outlet names.
Block Club, I'll click. Some outlet I don't know, I'll skip past it same as
before.

What I'd flat-out ignore: Archer Avenue. No offense to whoever's fighting
that fight, but that's not my side of town, I've never driven Mateo
through there let alone biked it, and I'm not going to read three
paragraphs of drama about a street I will probably bike exactly zero times
in my life. If that's on the list, I'm scrolling past it the second I see
the name, same as I scroll past a citywide stat that isn't about Avondale.

What I'd distrust, or at least raise an eyebrow at — "hand-maintained by
volunteers." I read that and my first thought isn't "untrustworthy," it's
"who?" Like, is this one guy? Is this the same people who do the crash map?
I don't know these people, I don't know if they ride my streets or if
they're way more into, like, the Archer Avenue side of things and just
glance at ours once a year. That's not the same as "the city says" being
suspicious because of money — this is a different kind of "who's telling
me this and do they actually know."

---

**Interviewer:** The status is written by the site's volunteers, not the
city, with a "last reviewed" date. Suppose the date says four months ago.
Do you trust it? What if it turns out the project got cancelled two months
ago and the site still said "funded"?

**Jess:** Four months, on its own — I'd probably still believe it, if I'm
honest, especially compared to a city page with no date on it at all, which
is basically every city page I've ever opened. At least a date is a date.
It's the same thing you told me about the fake obstruction layer a while
back, remember — I said I'd feel tricked if I found out something was made
up after I'd already screenshotted it and told myself "okay, this is real
now." A dated status from a volunteer isn't that, it's not pretending to be
something it's not, it's just maybe stale, like milk. I can smell stale
milk myself sometimes, I don't need someone to tell me it's four months
old, I just drink it a little more carefully.

But — cancelled two months ago and it still says "funded"? That one lands
different, and here's why: that's not a stale opinion, that's a wrong fact
sitting there looking exactly as confident as a true one. If I told Danny
"it's funded, I read it on the site" and then found out from Priya two
weeks later that it got killed, I'd feel dumb, and then I'd feel like the
site made me look dumb. That's the same "genuinely annoyed" feeling as the
fake obstruction thing, honestly, maybe worse, because there's no little
badge warning me this one might be pretend — it looks exactly like the
ones that are still true. I don't think one miss nukes the whole site for
me the way one bad headline didn't nuke the crash map — but if it happened
on a project I actually cared about, like something on my own route, that's
the one that'd make me go back to just asking Priya.

---

**Interviewer:** Your neighborhood's big project isn't on the list, but
the North Side's three projects are. What does that say to you about the
site?

**Jess:** Honestly? It says "of course." Not in a shocked way — in the way
where I already half expected it. It's the same feeling as the menu-money
thing, where you hear which wards get the nice new stuff and which wards
get told to wait. If I go looking and it's the 606, and something up by
Lincoln Square, and something in Wicker Park, and Avondale's not even
mentioned — I'm not going to think "the site is biased," I'm going to
think "yeah, that tracks, that's who gets written about." I'd probably
assume it's less about the site secretly favoring the North Side and more
about whoever's doing the volunteering just happening to live and ride over
there, same as I only really know about the 606 because it's basically in
my backyard. But either way, the takeaway for me is the same: this still
isn't really about my street. I'd check it maybe once, not find Avondale,
and then not think about the page again until somebody mentions our own
project by name.

---

**Interviewer:** Would you want this on the ward report you already saw,
on its own page, or somewhere else — or nowhere?

**Jess:** Not its own page — that's a whole separate thing I have to
remember exists, and I already told you, I don't have a checking-back
habit, I barely remember to check the pothole tracker for my own pothole.
If it's not attached to something I'm already looking at for another
reason, I'll never open it on purpose.

On the ward thing, sure, if it's right there when I'm already looking up
my alderman's menu money or whatever — like a little line, "proposed here:
[project], last reviewed [date]," same page, no extra click. Honestly what
I actually want is what I said before, the little digest — send it to me,
don't make me come get it. If the "here's what happened on your streets
this week" email or whatever also had a line like "606 extension: still in
design, no change" once a month, that I'd read for four seconds and move
on. But a whole standalone "Proposed & In Progress" page that I have to
navigate to on purpose? I'm not going to remember it's there past today.

---

**Interviewer:** Magic wand — one thing about "what's coming" you wish
anyone would tell you?

**Jess:** Tell me before the cones show up. That's it, that's the whole
wish. Last fall I turned onto our normal street with Mateo in the trailer
and there were orange barrels blocking half the lane out of nowhere, some
crew doing something to the curb, and I had to swing us out around a
turning car with my kid behind me because nobody told me that was
happening that week. I found out after, from Priya, that it had actually
been "planned" for like two months — somebody probably even wrote it on a
city PDF somewhere. I just never saw it, because why would I go looking
for something I didn't know to look for.

So if the wand does anything, it's that: not a map, not a status page I
have to go check — just, "hey, there's work starting on a street you
actually ride, this week." One text. That's the whole thing I actually
want out of all of this.
