## 1. Live-proposal inventory

**606 / Bloomingdale Trail eastward extension.** Official umbrella name is **The 606**; the elevated path itself is the **Bloomingdale Trail** (formally **Bloomingdale Trail Park**, per CDOT's own project page). The current proposal is CDOT's **Bloomingdale Trail Extension**, an eastward extension from the trail's current terminus at Ashland Avenue, under/through the Kennedy Expressway, to Elston Avenue — coordinated with the Lincoln Yards development site ([chicago.gov: Bloomingdale Trail Extension](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/cdot/supp_info/bloomingdale_trail.html); [Streetsblog Chicago, Aug 2023](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2023/08/08/the-planned-bloomingdale-trail-extension-is-short-but-it-would-get-you-past-the-kennedy-and-ashland-car-free)). As of a Jan 2025 Block Club update, construction had slipped to "end of 2026" start with completion **now expected late 2027**; estimated cost **$30–40 million**, funding still being assembled from federal/state/local sources, with Rep. Mike Quigley securing a partial congressional earmark (~$1.4M reported toward Phase II) ([Block Club Chicago, Jan 21 2025](https://blockclubchicago.org/2025/01/21/the-606s-bloomingdale-trail-extension-delayed-with-completion-now-expected-by-late-2027/); [Quigley press release](https://quigley.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/quigley-briefed-606-multiuse-trail-extension)). Status: **funded-in-part / in design (preliminary engineering wrapping up, final design phase selected)**, not yet under construction. There is no evidence of a westward extension into Belmont Cragin — that appeared as a plausible-sounding candidate in the task brief but WebSearch surfaced only the eastward Elston-Avenue extension as an active, named CDOT project; treat "westward extension" as **unverified/likely non-existent**.

**Archer Avenue Complete Streets / Traffic Safety Project (Brighton Park, 12th Ward).** Streetsblog's own tag taxonomy uses two label variants for the same project — "Archer Avenue Traffic Safety Project" and "Archer Traffic Safety Project" — both appearing across its 3 tagged articles from Mar–Apr 2026, alongside "Archer Guardians" (the pro-project resident group) and Ald. Julia Ramirez (12th Ward) ([Streetsblog tag feed, live-fetched](https://chi.streetsblog.org/tag/archer-avenue/feed/)). Installed protected bike lanes with concrete curb protection, pedestrian refuge islands, curb extensions, and new turn lanes; CDOT cited Archer/Kedzie as 57% of serious ped/cyclist injuries or fatalities in the corridor June 2024–2025, six fatalities 2020–2024 ([CBS Chicago](https://www.cbsnews.com/chicago/news/brighton-park-bike-lane-project-archer-avenue-traffic-complaints/)). After sustained resident protests over side-street traffic diversion and parking loss, the **city partially reversed course in April 2026**: restored 17 parking spaces across three sub-segments (Archer/Albany, Archer/Troy, Archer/Christiana) while explicitly keeping the concrete-protected bike lanes and stating there are "no plans to remove the project's core safety features" ([Block Club Chicago, Apr 30 2026](https://blockclubchicago.org/2026/04/30/city-removes-brand-new-concrete-bike-lanes-pedestrian-islands-in-brighton-park-amid-protests/); [Hoodline, Apr 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/04/brighton-park-bike-lane-brawl-city-rips-out-fresh-concrete-after-protests/)). Status: **installed / partially modified**, ongoing dueling-rally coverage as of late April 2026.

**Grand Avenue protected bike lanes (West Town, spanning 1st/27th/36th wards).** Multi-phase Complete Streets project; Phase 1 (36th Ward, near Kilbourn/Cicero-ish western stretch per 2023 Streetsblog coverage) built out with community-meeting support in 2023 ([Streetsblog, Jun 2023](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2023/06/30/big-turnout-in-support-of-upcoming-grand-ave-protected-bike-lanes-at-west-town-meeting)). **Phase 2**, extending toward downtown/Ogden Avenue, slipped to a 2026 start ([Block Club, Aug 18 2025](https://blockclubchicago.org/2025/08/18/second-phase-of-grand-avenue-bike-lane-street-upgrades-now-expected-in-2026/)) and by spring 2026 had become one of the highest-profile Complete Streets fights in the city: a business coalition and a West Town neighborhood group held **weekly rush-hour protests** near Ogden, met by counter-protesters; CDOT and Ald. Daniel La Spata (1st Ward) point to fewer severe crashes/injuries/speeding on completed segments as justification, with La Spata stating "there is no turning back" ([Streetsblog, Apr 22 2026](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2026/04/22/this-is-grand-scenes-from-chicagos-newest-weekly-dueling-rallies-against-and-for-a-complete-streets-project); [ABC7](https://abc7chicago.com/post/west-town-residents-push-back-bike-lane-pedestrian-project-despite-fewer-crashes-injuries-speeding-issues-finished-areas/19060230/); [CBS Chicago](https://www.cbsnews.com/chicago/news/coalition-opposes-bike-lane-construction-grand-avenue-west-town-downtown/)). Status: **Phase 1 complete, Phase 2 under construction/contested** as of mid-2026.

**Milwaukee Avenue (Kinzie–Chicago Ave) reconstruction, River West.** Distinct from the general "Milwaukee Line" main route — this is a specific 0.8-mile CDOT resurfacing project that added concrete curb protection to the existing bike lane, rebuilt the Milwaukee/Erie intersection, and added bus boarding islands; framed by the city as the "100th mile of bikeways" installed under Mayor Johnson ([chicago.gov press release, via WebSearch](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/cdot/provdrs/bike/news/2025/september/mayor-brandon-johnson--cdot-celebrate-100-miles-of-new-bikeways-.html); [Streetsblog "Eyes on the street," Oct 1 2025](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2025/10/01/eyes-on-the-street-taking-a-spin-on-the-improved-milwaukee-avenue-protected-bike-lanes-in-river-west-with-a-few-constructive-criticisms)). A construction closure (Apr 2025) required detour mapping while work was underway ([Streetsblog, Apr 4 2025](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2025/04/04/mapped-heres-how-to-bike-around-the-kinzie-to-chicago-ave-closure-of-the-milwaukee-avenue-hipster-highway)). ## 2. The 606 extension as worked example

The official project is CDOT's **Bloomingdale Trail Extension** (Ashland to Elston, under the Kennedy) — funded in part, in final design, construction targeted to start around the end of 2026 with completion in late 2027 ([Block Club, Jan 2025](https://blockclubchicago.org/2025/01/21/the-606s-bloomingdale-trail-extension-delayed-with-completion-now-expected-by-late-2027/)). A name-token matcher seeded with bare tokens like `"606"` or `"Bloomingdale"` would perform **badly**: Streetsblog's own `the-606` tag feed (12 items, live-fetched) is dominated by unrelated 606-trail news — a CPD gun-discharge-on-the-trail story, Lakefront Trail snow closures mis-tagged under "The 606," a COVID-era crowding piece, a High-Line-comparison essay — with only **one of twelve** items actually about the extension project. The `bloomingdale-trail` tag shows the identical pattern. A guessed narrower tag slug, `bloomingdale-trail-extension`, **404'd** as an addressable feed even though "Bloomingdale Trail Extension" appears as a category label on one article — meaning the matcher cannot rely on a clean, pre-filtered publisher tag here and must do its own phrase matching. The fix mirrors what OYL's `_route_matchers` already does for streets (`aggregate.py:1665-1683`): match multi-word phrases actually seen in headlines — "Bloomingdale Trail extension," "606 extension," "606's Bloomingdale Trail Extension," "606 Trail Extension" — rather than the bare corridor name, accepting that this yields very sparse, milestone-driven coverage (roughly one story a year) rather than a steady stream, which is honest given the project's multi-year design timeline.

## 3. Streetsblog tag-consistency check (RQ3)

Live-fetched five additional tag feeds beyond the two already verified in `docs/research/news-layer/evidence-feeds.md` (Milwaukee Ave, Augusta Blvd, Pat Dowell — all 100% on-topic). Results are genuinely mixed:

- `tag/archer-avenue/feed/` — **3/3 on-topic**, all 2026, tightly scoped to the Archer Traffic Safety Project.
- `tag/grand-avenue/feed/` — **6/6 on-topic** across 2019–2026, consistently about the Grand Ave bike-lane project (one 2019 item is adjacent — a Belmont Cragin transit survey that happens to mention Grand Ave — still corridor-relevant).
- `tag/dusable-lake-shore-drive/feed/` — **5/5 on-topic**, 2025–2026, all "Redefine the Drive" coverage.
- `tag/the-606/feed/` and `tag/bloomingdale-trail/feed/` — **~1/12 on-topic** for the extension specifically; the tag covers the whole 606/Bloomingdale ecosystem, not the extension project. This is the collision case detailed in §2.
- `tag/englewood-nature-trail/feed/` — only 2 items, both **2022**, despite active 2026 news coverage (Chicago YIMBY, Hoodline) — Streetsblog simply hasn't published/tagged Englewood Trail stories recently.
- `tag/weber-spur/feed/` — only 1 item, **2017**, about the Lincolnwood suburban segment — again stale relative to Feb 2026 funding news that ran on Block Club and Hoodline instead.
- `tag/bloomingdale-trail-extension/feed/` — **404**, confirming tag-slug guesses cannot be assumed to resolve (consistent with the caveat already on record in the prior evidence brief).

**Conclusion:** Streetsblog tags project-level coverage consistently *for projects it covers heavily and recently* (Archer, Grand, DLSD), but tag freshness/existence is not guaranteed — some real, actively-newsworthy projects (Englewood Nature Trail, Weber Spur) have stale or absent Streetsblog tags and their current coverage lives on outlets outside OYL's existing allowlist (Chicago YIMBY, Hoodline), which a Google News query could catch but the direct Streetsblog/Block Club feeds would miss.

## 4. Official structured sources, re-checked (RQ4)

**Still true:** there is no single machine-readable **geometry** dataset of CDOT's planned/proposed bikeways. CDOT's own tracking artifact is a **"Planned Bikeways Installations Tracker" spreadsheet** (categorizing segments as installed/underway/upcoming, linked from `chicago.gov/.../bike-program/planned-bike-projects.html`) — a structured *list*, but Streetsblog's own account of building a public map from it states a **volunteer had to manually geocode** CDOT's spreadsheet data to make it mappable ([Streetsblog, Jul 2023](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2023/07/11/geocoded-bikeway-installation-data)) — i.e., CDOT publishes attributes (street, segment description, status) but not ready-to-use line geometry. This is a meaningful refinement of the current `planned_routes.geojson` stub note ("CDOT publishes planned bikeways only as PDF maps") — it's actually a **spreadsheet-plus-static-map**, marginally better than "PDF only" but still not GeoJSON/shapefile-ready without manual work.

**New finding, worth flagging:** **CMAP's Bikeway Inventory System (BIS)** — already cited in `docs/research/user-needs/evidence/alt-data-sources.md` as a regional facility inventory — is described in its own metadata as covering **"existing, programmed, planned, and future" bike facilities** as ArcGIS feature layers/geodatabase across the seven-county region, accessible via `datahub.cmap.illinois.gov` ArcGIS REST services (WebSearch-derived; the actual field schema and per-record project-name attributes could **not** be confirmed live in this session — every WebFetch attempt against the ArcGIS Hub map/document pages returned only page-title text with no body content, likely because those pages render via client-side JS that this fetcher doesn't execute). **This is a real candidate for machine-readable planned-bikeway geometry that the original stub's framing may have missed** — but whether its "planned" layer includes attribute-level project names matchable to a hand-curated roster (e.g. does a BIS record for the Bloomingdale extension exist and carry a name field?) is **unverified** and should be checked directly against the ArcGIS REST endpoint (e.g. a `.../FeatureServer/{layer}/query?where=1=1&outFields=*&f=geojson` call) before relying on it, not through a browser-rendered Hub page.

**Chicago Cycling Strategy** (2023 update, PDF) remains a static planning document with a map graphic (existing vs. planned, blue vs. red) rather than exportable data ([chicago.gov PDF](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/cdot/bike/2023/2023_Chicago%20Cycling%20Update.pdf)). The Capital Improvement Program (CIP) is published as multi-year PDF books, not a bikeway-specific structured dataset. **Net answer for RQ4: still effectively PDFs/spreadsheets/static maps for CDOT's own planned-bikeway data; CMAP's BIS is the one plausible exception and needs direct REST verification, not assumed from search snippets.** The roster's geometry should stay hand-traced/absent as the stub already does, pending that BIS REST-endpoint check.

## 5. Status-from-news honesty check (RQ5)

Confirmed: **status cannot be derived deterministically from news text**, and the Archer Avenue saga is exactly the ambiguous case the task brief anticipated. Headlines and coverage in the April–May 2026 window are genuinely contradictory depending on outlet and which day's rally is being covered: Block Club and Hoodline framed it as the city "removing" bike lanes/pedestrian islands ("City Removes Brand-New Concrete Bike Lanes... Amid Protests," "City Rips Out Fresh Concrete After Protests"), which read as a full reversal if a matcher naively keyed off "removes"/"rips out." The actual substance, per the same reporting, is a **partial parking restoration in three sub-segments** while the concrete-protected bike lanes and pedestrian islands remained largely intact elsewhere on the corridor — a nuance that requires reading past the headline. Streetsblog separately published a piece explicitly critiquing a CBS Chicago report on the same project as "one-sided and misleading" ([Streetsblog, Dec 5 2025](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2025/12/05/deconstructing-cbss-extremely-one-sided-misleading-report-on-the-archer-complete-streets-project)) — direct outlet-vs-outlet disagreement about how to characterize the same facts on the same project, which is about as strong a confirmation as this kind of research can produce that **status must be a hand-curated field**, with the news layer supplying dated evidence links (including, ideally, links that visibly disagree with each other) rather than an inferred status value. The DLSD project shows the milder version of the same problem: "still revising the alternatives analysis" (Streetsblog, May 2026) is a status that only makes sense with an editorial gloss — a matcher would have no principled way to turn that into a `status` enum value like "proposed"/"blocked"/"in design."

- **Two Block Club Chicago article URLs** (the Apr 30 2026 Archer removal piece, the Axios roundup) — returned 403/429 on direct WebFetch in this session; cited via WebSearch snippet text only, consistent with the bot-detection pattern this repo has already documented for this outlet.

## Overall feasibility call: **GREEN** for the roster shape, **YELLOW** for name-token matching without per-project phrase tuning

A small hand-curated roster (name, corridor, status, official links, evidence-news via existing route-matcher machinery) is well supported by this research: every project investigated has enough real, dated, citable news coverage to make "what's being written about it" meaningful, and every project's status is genuinely contested/nuanced enough that hand-curation (not derivation) is the only honest approach — consistent with how `main_routes.json` already treats the roster as editorial. The **risk is entirely in the matcher**, not the roster concept: bare corridor-name tokens work well for projects with unambiguous names (Archer, Grand, DLSD's "Redefine the Drive") but **fail badly for the flagship worked example** (606/Bloomingdale) unless seeded with multi-word phrases distinct from routine trail coverage, and **miss entirely** for projects whose current news lives on outlets outside today's feed allowlist (Englewood Nature Trail, Weber Spur — both real, active, fundable, in-the-news projects that Streetsblog itself has stopped tagging). Recommend: (1) per-project curated phrase lists rather than single-token matching, mirroring the multi-pattern approach `_route_matchers` already uses for shared street names; (2) treat Streetsblog tag-feed existence as a bonus signal, not a requirement, since roughly half the projects checked here have stale or absent tags despite active real-world coverage; (3) verify the CMAP BIS REST endpoint directly before deciding the roster's geometry must stay fully hand-traced — it may not need to.
